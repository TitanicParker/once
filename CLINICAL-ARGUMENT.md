# Clinical Documentary Case — Five Expert Propositions

**Status:** documentary clinical argument. This document does not determine negligence, breach, legal causation, damages or specialist standard of care. Its purpose is to define the narrow propositions on which expert movement-disorders, biomechanical and podiatric opinion becomes decisive.

## Evidential control rule

The central proposition is deliberately narrower than “failure to reassess,” “failure to integrate,” or “path dependence” used as free-standing conclusions.

> **The issue for expert determination is whether the longitudinal evidence required an explicit re-evaluation of the original foot–neurology relationship, rather than continued management within the inherited mixed model.**

Two further controls apply throughout:

1. **Documentary absence is not proof of mental or undocumented absence.** The safe proposition is that the record does **not identify a comprehensive re-synthesis** of the original relationship. It is not that no clinician ever reconsidered it privately or orally.
2. **Treatment response establishes modifiability, not mechanism.** Procyclidine, pregabalin, Stalevo, Sinemet and Sinemet CR responses cannot by themselves identify the primary pain generator.

The argument therefore asks five expert propositions.

---

# EXPERT PROPOSITION 1 — What exactly remained unresolved in 2017?

## Documentary foundation

The presentation entered Neurology as an integrated but unexplained clinical problem.

The GP chronology moved from bilateral foot pain and abnormal gait into initiation difficulty, marked lower-limb tone, toe clawing, worsening dexterity and threatened employment. By late June the referral did not present “feet” and “neurology” as already separate disorders. It presented one deteriorating syndrome requiring neurological explanation. **CLIN-000018–000039; CLIN-000064–000085; CLIN-000847–000884.**

Hospital Neurology then documented both domains:

- severe young-onset Parkinsonism;
- a striking foot-drop-like gait considered suggestive of dystonic posture and expressly recorded as **“NOT FOOT DROP”**;
- pes planus and hammertoes;
- corns/calluses, burning and foot pain. **CLIN-001085–001149.**

Neurology did not state that the foot symptoms were neurologically unrelated. It stated that the leg symptoms, corns/calluses and burning might be completely separate or perhaps peripheral neuropathy, and concluded:

> **“Not clear.”**

**CLIN-001142–001149.**

The patient’s own early history supplied genuine mechanical evidence. He reported that foot pain had reduced after leaving bar work and spending less time standing, while leg tension persisted. **CLIN-000965–000976.**

Podiatry later repeatedly documented genuine focal structural pathology, especially recurrent left fifth-MTPJ callus/HD. In December 2018 the patient’s focal description — **“pain under the left side of my left foot”** — converged with objective left fifth-MTPJ callus and targeted offloading. **CLIN-002965–002973.**

## What this establishes

The 2017 problem was not “structural or neurological.” Both were real.

The unresolved issue was the **relationship** among:

- structural deformity and recurrent pressure lesions;
- pain;
- abnormal tone/posture/clawing;
- Parkinsonian/dystonic gait;
- loading;
- possible peripheral neuropathic symptoms.

The original uncertainty was therefore clinically genuine and not disproved merely because neurological treatment later helped parts of the foot/lower-limb phenotype.

## What this does not establish

It does not establish that:

- the April 2017 bilateral pain was the later left fifth-MTPJ lesion;
- the fifth-MTPJ lesion was caused by dystonia;
- all later burning pain was the same focal pain;
- Podiatry was inappropriate;
- levodopa should have been started in July 2017.

## Expert question

> **Was “Not clear” a reasonable provisional formulation in July 2017, and what later forms of evidence would ordinarily require that provisional relationship to be expressly reconsidered?**

# EXPERT PROPOSITION 2 — What management architecture was adopted in response?

## 2017–2021: deliberate levodopa deferral

Neurology expressly chose caution with ordinary dopamine agonist/levodopa treatment because of exceptionally young onset and concern about rapid dyskinesia and impulse-control effects. The contemporaneous policy was to use alternatives first and likely avoid the usual dopamine treatments **“for as long as possible.”** **CLIN-001166–001180.**

That was a real treatment decision, not a later invention.

At the same time, local/structural foot care was routed to Podiatry. The surviving hospital language asks Podiatry to review the foot issues and later asks for outpatient Chiropody/Podiatry. It does not assign Podiatry the neurological causal question. **CLIN-000343–000345; CLIN-001225–001229.**

This created two parallel but not necessarily contradictory pathways:

1. **Neurological strategy:** cautious symptomatic treatment while deliberately deferring usual dopamine replacement.
2. **Foot strategy:** Podiatry for genuine local/structural disease while the causal relationship remained unresolved.

The GP’s 6 July 2017 note that Podiatry would prioritise the case **“as Parkinson’s”** is important counterevidence against describing this as a total conceptual separation. It shows neurological context accompanied the practical referral. **CLIN-000236–000245.**

The stronger documentary point is therefore not that Podiatry was isolated from Parkinsonism, but that the surviving referral architecture does not identify who was responsible for resolving the unresolved **relationship** between the domains.

## Healy: compatible with caution, not clear endorsement of indefinite deferral

Galway expressly asked Prof Daniel Healy whether its wish to hold back levodopa should change. **CLIN-001720–001730.**

Healy replied that the patient **“probably has Park II,”** recommended testing PARK2 first, and stated that PARK II patients tend to manage for long periods with relatively low doses of dopamine replacement therapy but may become dyskinetic if over-used. **CLIN-001741–001760.**

The precise proposition is:

> **Healy’s advice was compatible with treatment caution but did not clearly endorse an indefinite or open-ended policy of levodopa deferral.**

It was not an instruction to commence levodopa immediately. Equally, it was not textually identical to Galway’s preference to postpone dopamine replacement for as long as possible.

No explicit contemporaneous adjudication of that distinction is identified.

## Post-2021: deferral ended; minimisation/constraint continued

This distinction is a formal hinge.

In September 2021 Sinemet was introduced because the patient felt undertreated, and the Parkinson nurse then proposed replacing Kemadrin with Sinemet because of ocular-pressure concerns. **CLIN-002330–002338.**

Therefore:

> **The literal levodopa-deferral period ended in 2021.**

After that, the management principle changed from **deferral** to **minimisation/constraint**. Neurology stated that it did not want Sinemet rising significantly and described a general aim in very young patients to spare Sinemet and keep dosing as minimal as possible. **CLIN-002351–002354.**

This prevents the inaccurate formulation that levodopa was “withheld for eight years.”

## Expert question

> **Was the architecture clinically coherent: 2017–2021 deliberate levodopa deferral plus parallel structural foot care, followed after 2021 by constrained/minimised levodopa exposure while the inherited mixed pain model remained available?**

# EXPERT PROPOSITION 3 — What later evidence materially affected the foot/lower-limb domain?

The later evidence does not point in only one direction. Its strength lies in the fact that both integrating and separating evidence accumulated.

## A. 2017 procyclidine: the narrow inference

The procyclidine withdrawal/reintroduction sequence establishes the minimum proposition needed for the case.

On treatment the patient described dramatic improvement in gait and physical function. During withdrawal, tension increased, the feet became restless and gait deteriorated. **CLIN-001770–001813.**

The only inference required is:

> **By 2017, the lower-limb/foot/gait domain was demonstrably neurological-treatment-state sensitive.**

Nothing further is needed.

It does **not** establish that:

- the recurrent fifth-MTPJ lesion was dystonic;
- procyclidine altered the structure of a corn;
- levodopa would necessarily reproduce the same benefit;
- all foot pain was neurological.

The local management response — restoring/escalating procyclidine within visual limits — was rational. The documentary question is whether the treatment-state information changed the original unresolved classification.

## B. 2018–2020: cross-domain evidence accumulates

PARK2 remained explicitly suspected. The fifth-MTPJ lesion remained objectively recurrent. GP later re-referred Podiatry describing foot issues in relation to increased tone, abnormal Parkinsonian gait, clawing and pain. In 2020 Neurology itself classified the foot-throw/foot-drop-like movement as **“actually parkinsonian”** because power was normal.

These observations make complete independence between “feet” and neurological movement increasingly difficult, while still not proving a single pain generator.

## C. 2021: the principal factual hinge — treatment substitution without identified structured foot measurement

Sinemet began in 2021 and Kemadrin/procyclidine was removed. This was a major change in neurological treatment design. **CLIN-002330–002354.**

The record does **not identify a structured pre/post assessment** of the already treatment-sensitive foot/lower-limb phenotype against:

- medication timing;
- dose onset;
- wearing-off;
- overnight absence;
- morning state / first steps;
- recurrent focal outside-left/fifth-MTPJ pain;
- clawing/curling or toe posture;
- gait and loading before and after dosing.

That is a documentary absence, not proof that no clinician ever asked about these matters.

The expert issue is therefore not whether immediate-release Sinemet was necessarily the wrong formulation. It is:

> **Whether a major treatment substitution in 2021 was adequately evaluated against an already-known neurological-treatment-state-sensitive lower-limb/foot domain.**

## D. 2022–2023: timing and OFF evidence becomes more explicit

In 2022 the feet took 20–25 minutes to “warm up” most mornings and were sore, while the patient varied Sinemet around functional demands.

In 2023 a Parkinson nurse recorded severe foot pain, reduced mobility/shuffling and worsening in the morning and later when Sinemet had not been taken, assessed the leg pain/shuffling as OFF-related, and commenced Stalevo after discussion with Neurology. **CLIN-002574–002582.**

Neurology separately interpreted the usual evening protective stance, lack of movement and short steps as typical OFF Parkinsonism and considered whether lack of levodopa was contributing. **CLIN-002449–002474.**

These observations are consistent with a clinically relevant treatment-coverage component. They cannot establish that the same component existed unchanged in 2021.

## E. 2023 counterevidence: credibility safeguard, not inconvenience

The same 2023 record contains evidence that strongly resists a unitary neurological-pain theory:

- severe bilateral **“burning”** feet and lower legs;
- extreme burden described by the GP as causing a **“death wish”**;
- high-dose pregabalin with only partial/incomplete control;
- the patient’s report that he stopped Parkinson medication for about ten days without perceiving a Parkinsonian difference. **CLIN-002637–002641; CLIN-002681–002684.**

This counterevidence is central to the credibility of the argument.

It demonstrates that the case is not reverse-engineered around the later Sinemet CR response. A genuinely mixed model remains necessary.

## F. 2024 pain/posture dissociation

By 2024 Lyrica had removed much of the foot pain, particularly on the right, while toe curling persisted. This is direct evidence that pain and posture were not one variable.

The foot domain therefore required separate tracking of:

- focal pressure pain;
- broader burning pain;
- clawing/curling/dystonic posture;
- gait architecture;
- standing/loading tolerance;
- medication state.

## G. 2025: explicit temporal treatment model

By 2025 the patient had substantially self-reduced Lyrica. Neurology recorded feet mainly left, **“murder in the feet,”** first 150 steps **“torture,”** and relief once Parkinson medication reached the system. **CLIN-002767–002800.**

Neurology then explicitly mapped the overnight medication interval, weighed additional levodopa benefit against dyskinesia, considered Lyrica as an alternative lever, and prescribed bedtime Sinemet CR. The later complaint response recorded **“profound relief currently.”** **CLIN-003263–003270.**

This is strong evidence for a medication-state component of the later foot experience.

It does not prove:

- the primary pain generator was Parkinsonian;
- the original focal lesion was neurological;
- CR should have been prescribed years earlier;
- the 2025 response can be back-projected unchanged into 2021.

## Expert question

> **Taken together, how strongly did the 2017–2025 sequence establish that clinically important parts of the foot/lower-limb domain were neurological-treatment-state sensitive while other pain components remained structural, neuropathic or otherwise separable?**

# EXPERT PROPOSITION 4 — Was the later evidence integrated into a comprehensive re-evaluation, or mainly managed locally within the inherited model?

This is the principal documentary proposition.

The record shows extensive **local management change**:

- procyclidine restored/escalated after withdrawal;
- dopamine agonists introduced and constrained by chorea/behavioural effects;
- Sinemet introduced in 2021;
- Stalevo used for OFF/undertreatment phenomena;
- pregabalin escalated for severe pain;
- Sinemet timing altered;
- Sinemet CR introduced for the overnight/morning pattern.

The record therefore cannot support “management never changed.”

The narrower question is whether those changes were accompanied by an explicit comprehensive re-evaluation of the original relationship among structural pathology, focal pain, neuropathic pain, dystonia/posture, loading, gait and medication state.

## Documentary formulation

The safe finding is:

> **The record does not identify a comprehensive re-synthesis of the original 2017 foot–neurology relationship comparable in explicitness to the repeated local treatment changes.**

That is not proof that clinicians never reconsidered the issue mentally, orally or in undocumented discussion.

## Pregabalin genealogy: contemporaneous purpose versus later narrative

The contemporaneous 2023 record states that the patient was **commenced on Lyrica by his GP** for severe foot/leg pain. **CLIN-002681–002684.**

By 2025 Neurology genuinely used Lyrica and Sinemet as alternative treatment levers. More Sinemet was expected to give some pain relief but increase dyskinesia, so increasing Lyrica was considered partly to avoid additional Parkinson medication. **CLIN-002787–002800.**

The later complaint response states that Lyrica was **introduced** for neuropathic pain **“in part to try to spare further Sinemet increase.”** **CLIN-003265.**

The correct evidential classification is formulaic:

> **Later Sinemet-sparing use of pregabalin is documented; Sinemet-sparing as part of the original purpose of pregabalin initiation is not established by the contemporaneous initiation record.**

This is **retrospective purpose attribution / possible reconstruction**, not proven falsity.

It illustrates why contemporaneous purpose and later explanatory narrative should not be silently merged.

## 2025 complaint-response genealogy: continuity, not simple contradiction

The strongest point is not that the 2025 complaint response was “wrong.”

The response expressly:

- accepts Sinemet-related pain relief;
- preserves the proposition that Parkinson’s may magnify pain **“of whatever cause”** rather than directly causing all pain;
- reaches back to the 2017 levodopa/dopamine-sparing rationale;
- uses that rationale to explain the later treatment course and continued caution. **CLIN-003239–003268.**

This makes the complaint response probative as evidence of **continuity of the management architecture in the clinician’s own retrospective account**.

By 2025 the literal non-use of levodopa had long ended, but the higher-order principles remained recognisable:

1. dopaminergic exposure should be constrained because of young-age dyskinesia/behavioural risk;
2. alternative symptom routes should be used where possible;
3. primary foot pain may remain attributable to structural/peripheral/other causes even when Parkinson state modifies its severity or expression.

That model may be clinically correct. The documentary question is whether it was ever formally returned to an open state and re-tested against the accumulated cross-domain evidence.

## Strongest benign interpretation

A benign account is coherent:

- structural pathology was real;
- Podiatry was appropriate;
- early mechanical evidence existed;
- procyclidine caused visual toxicity;
- dopamine agonists caused choreiform/behavioural problems;
- levodopa later produced dyskinesia at low exposure;
- pregabalin materially helped pain;
- pain and toe posture were partly dissociable;
- OFF phenomena were recognised and treated as they became clearer;
- Sinemet CR was introduced when the overnight pattern became sufficiently explicit.

On this view, clinicians did not need a single dramatic “global reset.” They reasonably refined separate components of a genuinely mixed syndrome.

## Strongest path-dependent interpretation

The competing interpretation is narrower than “they ignored contrary evidence.” They did not. They repeatedly recognised evidence and acted on it locally.

The stronger proposition is:

> **A provisional 2017 mixed model may have become a durable organising frame capable of absorbing later integrating evidence without requiring the original classification itself to return to an open state.**

Thus:

- structural lesion → Podiatry;
- burning pain → neuropathic pathway;
- neurological drug changes feet → treatment modulation;
- Parkinsonian foot movement → motor component;
- OFF pain/shuffling → local levodopa adjustment;
- Lyrica helps → pain pathway;
- Sinemet helps → reduced experience of pain “of whatever cause”;
- CR profoundly helps → successful modulation while primary generator remains separate.

Because that architecture can accommodate both integrating and separating observations, the key expert question is not whether it was internally coherent, but whether it remained **sufficient** as the evidence accumulated.

## Expert question

> **Does the longitudinal record show adequate re-evaluation of the original foot–neurology relationship, or principally repeated local management within an inherited mixed model whose underlying classification remained substantially intact?**

# EXPERT PROPOSITION 5 — At what point, if any, would reasonable movement-disorders practice have required reopening the original classification?

This is the ultimate expert question and should not be answered documentarily.

The evidence that may bear on the threshold includes:

1. **2017:** severe Parkinsonism plus structural foot disease; causal relationship expressly **“Not clear.”**
2. **2017:** neurological treatment/withdrawal materially changes feet, gait and tension.
3. **2017:** Healy considers probable PARK2 and describes low-dose dopamine replacement as potentially sustainable but dyskinesia-prone if over-used.
4. **2017–2019:** recurrent left fifth-MTPJ structural pathology remains real.
5. **2019:** GP re-links foot problems to increased tone, abnormal Parkinsonian gait, clawing and pain.
6. **2020:** Neurology classifies the abnormal foot throw as Parkinsonian.
7. **2021:** Sinemet replaces the procyclidine-centred strategy, but no structured foot-by-dose assessment is identified.
8. **2022:** morning foot warm-up/soreness appears.
9. **2023:** nurse and Neurology identify OFF/undertreatment phenomena affecting leg pain, shuffling and evening movement.
10. **2023:** strong counterevidence simultaneously persists — bilateral burning, pregabalin benefit, reported ten-day cessation without perceived Parkinsonian difference.
11. **2024:** pain improves while toe curling persists, separating symptom dimensions.
12. **2025:** morning/overnight pattern is explicitly mapped; feet improve after Parkinson medication; Sinemet CR produces reported profound relief.
13. **2025:** complaint response preserves the inherited separate-cause/magnification model while retrospectively defending the original dopamine-sparing rationale.
14. **2026:** two pathogenic PRKN variants confirm Parkin-related Parkinsonism/dystonia. **CLIN-003220–003228.**

The 2026 genetics result cannot be back-projected into 2017 knowledge. It strongly validates the early PARK2 branch but does not establish the mechanism of the corn, burning pain or every later foot symptom.

## What the expert must decide

The expert should be asked to identify, if possible, a **threshold**, not merely to endorse or reject the whole historical course.

> **At what point, if any, did the accumulated evidence make continued management within the inherited mixed structural/peripheral/neurological model insufficient without an explicit integrated re-evaluation?**

If such a threshold existed, the expert should specify what a reasonable reassessment would ordinarily have included, for example:

- structured ON/OFF examination;
- symptom-by-dose diary;
- medication-onset and wearing-off mapping;
- overnight/morning assessment;
- separate measures of focal pain, burning pain, toe posture and gait;
- neurological–podiatric biomechanical review;
- reassessment of the peripheral-neuropathy hypothesis;
- renewed movement-disorders/genetic review.

The expert should also address whether the substantial treatment risks — ocular toxicity, behavioural/impulse effects, chorea and levodopa dyskinesia — reasonably justified continued caution even if a reassessment threshold had been crossed.

---

# Strongest surviving formulation

The case that survives adversarial testing is therefore:

> **An initially reasonable unresolved mixed structural–neurological formulation led to parallel foot and neurological management and deliberate levodopa deferral. Literal levodopa deferral ended in 2021 and was replaced by constrained/minimised dopaminergic treatment. Across the same period, increasingly specific evidence showed that clinically important parts of the foot/lower-limb domain were neurological-treatment-state sensitive, while other pain components remained structurally or analgesically separable. The record documents repeated local treatment adaptation but does not identify a comparably explicit comprehensive re-synthesis of the original foot–neurology relationship. The specialist question is whether, and at what point, reasonable movement-disorders practice required that relationship to be explicitly re-evaluated rather than continuing to manage new evidence within the inherited mixed model.**

This formulation does not depend on proving bad faith, a unitary pain mechanism, an obviously wrong 2017 decision, inappropriate Podiatry care, eight years of levodopa withholding, or an inevitable earlier Sinemet CR solution.
