# Clinical Documentary Case — Strongest Surviving Argument

**Status:** documentary clinical argument. It does not determine negligence, breach, legal causation or a specialist standard of care.

## 1. The central issue

The strongest case is not that every historical foot symptom was caused by Parkinsonism, that Podiatry treatment was inappropriate, or that levodopa necessarily should have begun in July 2017.

The stronger case is longitudinal:

> **An expressly unresolved mixed structural–neurological presentation was converted into workable parallel management pathways. The central question is whether, and when, accumulating evidence required the original relationship and management architecture to be explicitly reopened rather than repeatedly updated only at component level.**

## 2. The presentation entered Neurology as an integrated but unexplained problem

The GP chronology moved from bilateral foot pain and abnormal gait into initiation difficulty, marked tone, toe clawing, dexterity loss and threatened employment. By late June the referral did not present “feet” and “neurology” as already separate disorders. It presented one deteriorating syndrome requiring neurological explanation. **CLIN-000018–000039; CLIN-000064–000085; CLIN-000847–000884.**

## 3. July 2017 correctly preserved more than one explanation

Hospital Neurology documented severe Parkinsonism and a striking foot-drop-like/dystonic gait while also recording pes planus, hammertoes, corns/calluses and foot pain. It did not claim the foot symptoms were neurologically unrelated. It said the relationship was **“Not clear.”** **CLIN-001085–001149.**

That uncertainty was defensible. The patient’s own history supplied mechanical evidence: foot pain had reduced when he left bar work and spent less time standing, while leg tension persisted. **CLIN-000965–000976.**

The correct starting point is therefore mixed pathology, not retrospective certainty.

## 4. Podiatry was justified — but it solved a service problem more clearly than the causal problem

Podiatry repeatedly documented real pathology, especially a recurrent left fifth-MTPJ callus/HD. In Dec 2018 the patient’s focal complaint — “pain under the left side of my left foot” — converged with objective fifth-MTPJ callus and targeted offloading. This is strong evidence for a genuine persistent focal structural/mechanical signal. **CLIN-002965–002973.**

The surviving hospital referral language, however, asks Podiatry to review foot issues; it does not task Podiatry with determining whether dystonic/Parkinsonian gait altered loading or whether pain was structural, neuropathic, dystonic or mixed. **CLIN-000343–000345; CLIN-001225–001229.**

This does not make Podiatry inappropriate. It means the **service destination** was clearer than the ownership of the unresolved causal relationship.

## 5. The management plan was deliberate and durable

Neurology expressly chose to defer usual dopamine agonist/levodopa treatment because of extremely young onset and concern for rapid dyskinesia and impulse-control effects. The strategy was to use alternatives first and likely avoid the usual dopamine treatments “as long as possible.” **CLIN-001166–001180.**

The rationale was not invented later. It is contemporaneous and clinically substantial. Subsequent chorea, behavioural effects and levodopa dyskinesia strengthened its benign basis.

But this also created an organising frame: neurological symptoms would be treated cautiously through non-levodopa strategies while structural/local feet were managed separately.

## 6. Healy did not destroy the plan, but neither did he simply restate it

Galway asked Healy whether its wish to hold back levodopa should change. Healy replied that the patient “probably has Park II,” recommended PARK2 testing and described PARK II patients as often managing for long periods with relatively low doses of dopamine replacement while becoming dyskinetic if over-used. **CLIN-001720–001760.**

This is compatible with caution. It is not an order for immediate levodopa. Yet it is also not textually identical to an open-ended policy of holding levodopa back for as long as possible. The record does not identify an explicit adjudication of that distinction.

## 7. Procyclidine created an early cross-domain natural experiment

Before levodopa, Neurology’s own treatment regime showed that the lower-limb/foot domain was neurologically treatment-state sensitive. On procyclidine the patient described near-miraculous global function; on withdrawal, tension built, feet became restless and gait fell apart while vision improved. **CLIN-001770–001813.**

This does **not** prove that a fifth-MTPJ lesion was dystonic or that levodopa would reproduce the same effect. It establishes something narrower but important: the feet/gait/tension domain could change with neurological treatment state.

The local response was rational: restore/escalate procyclidine within visual limits. What is not identified is a contemporaneous global question: what does this mean for the original “Not clear” foot–neurology allocation?

## 8. 2018–2020 increased the pressure for integration without eliminating the benign case

PARK2 remained explicitly suspected. The recurrent fifth-MTPJ lesion remained objectively real. GP later re-referred Podiatry describing feet in relation to increased tone, abnormal Parkinsonian gait and clawing. In 2020 Neurology itself classified the foot-throw movement as “actually parkinsonian.”

These events make complete independence increasingly difficult. They still do not prove that all pain had a neurological generator.

## 9. 2021 is the pivotal treatment-design question

Literal levodopa deferral ended when Sinemet began in Sep 2021 for undertreatment and then replaced Kemadrin because of ocular-pressure concerns. **CLIN-002330–002354.**

But the higher-level risk policy persisted: Neurology did not want Sinemet rising significantly and stated that in very young people the goal was to spare it and keep dosing minimal. **CLIN-002351–002354.**

The record does not identify a structured prospective assessment of the already treatment-sensitive foot phenotype against:
- dose onset;
- wearing-off;
- overnight absence;
- morning first steps;
- focal outside-left pain;
- clawing/curling;
- gait/loading.

The defensible criticism is therefore **not** that immediate-release Sinemet was necessarily the wrong formulation. It is the expert question whether a major treatment substitution was adequately evaluated against an already-known lower-limb response domain.

## 10. Later chronology is consistent with an unmeasured coverage component — but does not prove one retrospectively

In 2022 the feet needed 20–25 minutes to “warm up” most mornings. In 2023 a nurse assessed leg pain/shuffling as OFF-related and initiated Stalevo after consultation; Neurology separately interpreted usual evening protective stance/lack movement/short steps as typical OFF Parkinson’s and considered insufficient levodopa. **CLIN-002574–002582; CLIN-002449–002474.**

These facts are consistent with a clinically relevant treatment-coverage component. They cannot establish that the same component was present unchanged in 2021.

## 11. The 2023 record prevents a unitary neurological-pain theory

The same year contains severe bilateral burning feet/lower legs, extreme burden, high-dose pregabalin and the patient’s report of ten days off Parkinson medication without perceived Parkinsonian difference. **CLIN-002637–002641; CLIN-002681–002684.**

This counterevidence matters. It supports a genuinely mixed model and prevents the later Sinemet response from being used as a retrospective master key.

## 12. Pain and posture separated

By 2023–24 Lyrica had removed much of the foot pain, while toe curling remained. That is direct evidence that pain and posture were not one variable. A reasonable longitudinal model had to distinguish focal pressure pain, broad burning pain, curling/dystonia, gait architecture and medication state.

## 13. Pregabalin shows why retrospective management narratives require genealogy

The contemporaneous 2023 record says the patient was **commenced on Lyrica by his GP** for severe foot/leg pain. **CLIN-002681–002684.**

By 2025 Neurology genuinely used Lyrica and Sinemet as alternative levers: more Sinemet was expected to give some pain relief but worsen dyskinesia, so increasing Lyrica was considered partly to avoid increasing Parkinson medication. **CLIN-002787–002800.**

The complaint response later states that Lyrica was **introduced** “in part to try to spare further Sinemet increase.” **CLIN-003265.**

The later balancing strategy is well documented. The original purpose attribution is not. The contemporaneous initiation record instead identifies GP treatment of severe pain. The safest classification is **retrospective purpose attribution / possible reconstruction**, not proven falsity.

## 14. 2025 finally produces a fully articulated foot-centred temporal treatment model

By 2025 the patient had self-reduced Lyrica from 600 mg/day to 50 mg TDS; Neurology expressly stated it had not advised the reduction. The clinical note records feet mainly left, “murder in the feet,” first 150 steps “torture,” and relief once Parkinson medication reaches the system. **CLIN-002767–002800.**

Neurology then explicitly maps the overnight interval, weighs further levodopa benefit against dyskinesia, considers Lyrica, and prescribes nighttime Sinemet CR. The later complaint response records “profound relief currently.” **CLIN-003263–003270.**

This is strong therapeutic evidence for a medication-state component of the later foot experience. It is not proof that the primary pain generator was Parkinsonian or that CR should have been used years earlier.

## 15. The complaint reopened the dispute more than it reopened the historical model

The 2025 complaint response acknowledges Sinemet pain benefit, but retains the proposition that Parkinson’s can magnify pain “of whatever cause.” It then reaches back to the original 2017 levodopa-sparing rationale and uses it to explain the later treatment course. **CLIN-003239–003268.**

Thus the response is best classified as a **retrospective defence of prior policy**, not a documented abandonment of the original architecture.

The literal non-use of levodopa had ended. The higher-order assumptions remained:
1. dopamine should be minimised because of young-age toxicity;
2. alternative symptom routes should be used where possible;
3. primary foot pain may remain structurally/peripherally attributable even when Parkinson state modifies its expression.

## 16. PRKN confirmation changes diagnostic certainty, not the evidential rules

In Feb 2026 two pathogenic Parkin variants were confirmed and Parkinsonism/dystonia attributed to compound heterozygous PRKN mutations. **CLIN-003220–003228.**

This strongly validates the early PARK2 branch and makes later purely “idiopathic” historical summaries incomplete. It does not prove that the corn was dystonic, that burning pain was PRKN-mediated, that Podiatry was wrong, or that earlier levodopa/CR was mandatory.

## 17. The strongest benign explanation

The strongest defence is coherent: mixed pathology existed; Podiatry was appropriate; the patient initially distinguished mechanical pain from tension; drug toxicities were substantial and later validated caution; pregabalin helped pain; levodopa produced dyskinesia; OFF states were recognised and treated as they became clear; CR was introduced when the overnight pattern was sufficiently explicit. A dual model of genuine structural/neuropathic pain worsened by Parkinsonian OFF state can explain much of the record without error.

## 18. The strongest path-dependent interpretation

The competing interpretation is not that clinicians ignored all contrary evidence. They repeatedly recognised it and acted locally.

The stronger proposition is that a **provisional 2017 solution to uncertainty became a durable organising architecture**. It could absorb almost every later event:
- structural lesion → Podiatry;
- burning pain → neuropathic pathway;
- neurological drug changes feet → modulation;
- Parkinsonian foot movement → motor component;
- OFF pain/shuffling → local levodopa adjustment;
- Lyrica helps → pain pathway;
- Sinemet helps → magnification of pain “of whatever cause”;
- CR profoundly helps → successful modulation while primary generator remains separate.

Because the model could accommodate both confirming and contradicting observations, the critical expert question becomes:

> **What evidence would ever have required the original “Not clear” structural/peripheral/neurological classification itself to return to an open state?**

The record does not provide a clear prospective rule.

## 19. What actually survives adversarial testing

The durable case is therefore narrower and stronger:

> **An initially reasonable unresolved mixed formulation was followed by repeated local treatment adaptation. Over time, increasingly specific evidence showed that parts of the same foot/lower-limb domain were neurologically and pharmacologically state-dependent, while other parts remained structural or analgesically separable. No comparably explicit comprehensive historical re-synthesis of the original relationship is identified. The central specialist question is whether, and at what point, reasonable movement-disorders care required such an integrated reassessment despite genuine structural disease and genuine treatment toxicity.**

That proposition does not depend on proving bad faith, a unitary pain mechanism, an obviously wrong 2017 decision, or an inevitable earlier CR solution.
