# EXPERT-QUESTION AUDIT — SHARPEST QUESTIONS ARISING FROM THE FULL RECORD

## Scope and evidential controls

This audit is framed for an independent movement-disorders / neurology expert. It does **not** decide negligence, breach, causation, motive, dishonesty, or what treatment definitely should have been given.

The questions below are built from the longitudinal documentary sequence, with particular weight given to:

- the 2017–2018 procyclidine treatment / withdrawal / reintroduction sequence;
- the documented rapid return of the outside-left-foot / corn-associated phenomenon during withdrawal;
- recurrent structural Podiatry findings;
- the 2022 morning foot “warm-up” pattern;
- the March 2023 severe burning-pain crisis and high-dose pregabalin history;
- later 2023 OFF-related pain / shuffling and Stalevo response;
- the 2024 separation between pain improvement and persistent toe curling;
- the 2025 overnight-gap hypothesis, Sinemet CR trial, and reported profound relief;
- the 2025 complaint response’s retrospective reconstruction of the earlier management strategy;
- the much stronger visible longitudinal carry-forward of dyskinesia / behavioural-risk evidence than of the earlier foot-specific medication-state evidence.

Throughout this audit:

- documented benefit ≠ proven benefit;
- later summary ≠ contemporaneous fact;
- pregabalin use ≠ proof of neuropathy;
- treatment failure ≠ exclusion of neuropathy;
- neurological modifiability ≠ proof of a neurological pain generator;
- later Sinemet CR success ≠ proof that it would have worked earlier;
- absence of later mention ≠ proof that earlier reasoning was forgotten;
- expert questions test clinical significance rather than assume the answer.

---


## Threshold-date instruction for the expert

Before answering the ranked questions, identify three dates or decision points explicitly:

1. **Earliest clinically meaningful modifiability threshold:** the earliest date at which the contemporaneous record established that at least part of the foot symptom complex was patient-specifically neurologically medication-state modifiable.
2. **Earliest re-synthesis threshold:** the earliest later date at which the accumulated evidence made it clinically reasonable to require that earlier modifiability finding to be expressly re-integrated into the working causal and management model.
3. **Earliest timing/formulation/coverage threshold:** the earliest date at which a structured test of dopaminergic timing, formulation, absorption conditions or overnight coverage became clinically reasonable, distinct from simply increasing total daily levodopa dose.

For each threshold, the expert should:

- identify the contemporaneous evidence available at that date;
- explain what changed in the evidential state;
- state whether the threshold was crossed, not crossed, or cannot be determined;
- distinguish possibility from probability;
- avoid using later treatment success as if it had been known prospectively.

This threshold analysis should precede any global opinion on whether management was reasonable.

# 1. Ranked expert questions

## Question 1 — What did the 2017–2018 procyclidine withdrawal/rechallenge establish about the foot syndrome itself?

**Decision period:** September/October 2017 through April 2018.

**Evidence available at that time:**

- Neurology had initially left the relationship between Parkinsonism and the corns/calluses/burning foot symptoms as **“Not clear.”**
- The patient’s contemporaneous October 2017 email records marked global physical benefit on the neurological regimen, followed during planned procyclidine withdrawal by increasing tension, restless feet, and deterioration of gait.
- The April 2018 Neurology note later records that, during the two-week procyclidine withdrawal, the **first thing noticed changing rapidly was the return at the outside-left-foot / corn site**, together with becoming more “shifty” and “unrelaxed.”
- Structural foot pathology remained independently documented by Podiatry.

**Proposition for the expert to decide:**

Did this sequence establish only general motor responsiveness to neurological treatment, or did it create a clinically meaningful patient-specific finding that **at least part of the foot symptom complex itself was neurologically medication-state modifiable**, even though the underlying pain generator remained unresolved?

**Why the answer matters:**

This is the hinge between the original “Not clear” causal proposition and every later episode of recurrent foot pain. If the withdrawal/rechallenge had specific clinical significance for the feet, then later foot-pain assessment should be tested against whether that fact remained an active premise.

**Question type:** Mechanism / treatment-strategy / standard-of-care.

## Question 2 — Once foot-specific neurological modifiability had been demonstrated, what would appropriate clinical carry-forward have required when the foot problem recurred?

**Decision period:** 2018 onward, especially 2019, 2022, March 2023, later 2023, 2024, and 2025.

**Evidence available at those later points:**

- A patient-specific neurological medication-state effect involving the original foot region had already entered the Neurology record.
- Podiatry continued to document structural / mechanical pathology.
- Later records introduced or reintroduced different framings: corn/callus, possible neuropathy, neuropathic pain, OFF-related pain, dystonia / toe curling, and Parkinson-related pain amplification.

**Proposition for the expert to decide:**

Should the earlier medication-state finding have remained an explicit standing premise whenever disabling foot pain recurred, such that later clinicians should have either incorporated it, re-tested it, or expressly explained why it no longer altered the working model?

**Why the answer matters:**

The record shows strong local updating in 2017–2018 but weaker visible longitudinal use of the specific focal foot finding. This question tests whether that documentary discontinuity had clinical significance without assuming anyone forgot or ignored the earlier evidence.

**Question type:** Standard-of-care / treatment-strategy.

## Question 3 — Did the 2022 morning “warm-up” pattern, when read in light of the earlier withdrawal evidence, cross a threshold for re-synthesis or a timed treatment-state test?

**Decision period:** 2022 Neurology review.

**Evidence available at that time:**

- The patient reported that most mornings the feet took approximately **20–25 minutes to warm up**, with soreness around corns/calluses and a question of whether the pain was neuralgic.
- Sinemet was already in use and was adjusted according to expected functional demand.
- Earlier Neurology documentation had shown rapid foot-state change during procyclidine withdrawal.
- Dyskinesia sensitivity had already been demonstrated and remained a real treatment constraint.

**Proposition for the expert to decide:**

Did the reproducible morning pattern, when combined with the already documented 2017–2018 foot-specific medication-state effect and the fact that Sinemet was now in use, cross the threshold for a structured test of medication onset, timing, formulation, absorption conditions, overnight under-coverage, dystonia, loading, central pain modulation, or another state-dependent mechanism? If not, what further evidence would reasonably have been required?

**Why the answer matters:**

This question avoids hindsight. It asks whether the evidence **available in 2022**, taken with the earlier patient-specific withdrawal finding, was enough to justify re-testing medication-state dependence before the severe 2023 crisis.

**Question type:** Standard-of-care / treatment-strategy.

## Question 4 — What reassessment was clinically indicated during the March 2023 severe foot-pain crisis?

**Decision period:** March 2023 urgent GP referral and Parkinson nurse / Neurology involvement.

**Evidence available at that time:**

- Severe bilateral burning foot / lower-leg pain with major functional impact.
- Pregabalin had been started by the GP and escalated through clinicians to approximately **600 mg/day**.
- The GP referral described some initial benefit but later only marginal control.
- A near-contemporaneous nurse record documented debilitating pain and no quality of life despite the high dose.
- Structural foot pathology had a long prior history.
- Earlier neurological medication-state modifiability of the feet had been documented.
- The patient himself believed at that point that the severe pain was not Parkinson-related and reported a roughly ten-day Parkinson-medication interruption without a perceived difference “from a Parkinson’s perspective.”

**Proposition for the expert to decide:**

Did this combination require a full reconstruction of the longitudinal foot-pain model — structural, neuropathic-type, dystonic, medication-state and other possibilities — rather than management of isolated components without explicit re-synthesis?

**Why the answer matters:**

March 2023 is the point at which multiple competing facts converged. It tests whether the earlier medication-state evidence, high-dose pregabalin inadequacy, structural history, and new burning phenotype should have been jointly reconsidered.

**Question type:** Standard-of-care / differential diagnosis / treatment-strategy.

## Question 5 — What can the pregabalin history legitimately establish, and what can it not establish?

**Decision period:** Early 2023 through 2025.

**Evidence available across the sequence:**

- GP-originated pregabalin for severe burning / neuropathic-type pain, starting at low dose.
- Escalation through multiple clinicians to approximately 600 mg/day.
- Severe pain documented despite 600 mg/day at some points.
- Later Neurology documents attribute to the patient substantial or near-substantial benefit at other points.
- Severe recurrence again occurred while on high-dose pregabalin.
- Later documentation records pain improvement while toe curling persisted.
- The patient subsequently reduced pregabalin himself, after which major pain was again prominent.

**Proposition for the expert to decide:**

Does this history support only **variable analgesic modulation**, or does it provide meaningful diagnostic evidence for peripheral neuropathy? How should severe high-dose inadequacy be reconciled with later documented periods of benefit?

**Why the answer matters:**

The answer determines how much weight should legitimately have been given to pregabalin as a diagnostic clue, as a management strategy, and as an alternative to dopaminergic adjustment.

**Question type:** Mechanism / differential diagnosis / treatment-strategy.

## Question 6 — Was pregabalin clinically separable from the dopaminergic-treatment question once the feet had already shown neurological medication-state responsiveness?

**Decision period:** 2023–2025.

**Evidence available at that time:**

- Earlier foot-specific modifiability during procyclidine withdrawal.
- GP-originated pregabalin treatment for severe burning pain.
- Later OFF-related pain / shuffling observations.
- Stalevo/Sinemet states later associated with pain change.
- Ongoing concern about dyskinesia and other dopaminergic complications.

**Proposition for the expert to decide:**

Could pregabalin reasonably be used as the principal pain-management alternative to further dopaminergic treatment **without explicitly incorporating or re-testing the earlier evidence that the foot syndrome itself could vary with neurological medication state**?

**Why the answer matters:**

The 2025 complaint response retrospectively places pregabalin partly inside a levodopa-sparing strategy. This question tests whether such a strategy was clinically valid only if the pain’s demonstrated medication-state sensitivity was simultaneously recognised.

**Question type:** Treatment-strategy / standard-of-care.

## Question 7 — Did reasonable “caution” require symmetrical attention to both dopaminergic adverse-effect risk and the competing risk of undertreating a disabling medication-sensitive symptom?

**Decision period:** 2017 onward, particularly 2018–2025.

**Evidence available at those times:**

- Strong patient-specific evidence of dyskinesia / chorea / behavioural sensitivity to dopaminergic treatment.
- Strong and repeated clinician concern about young age and long-term dopaminergic complications.
- Earlier patient-specific foot-state response to neurological medication withdrawal.
- Later recurrence of disabling pain and later confirmation that Sinemet state could materially alter the pain experience.

**Proposition for the expert to decide:**

When balancing dopaminergic risk, should the clinician also have treated **undertreatment or inadequate temporal coverage of a disabling, already medication-sensitive symptom** as a competing risk? What would a balanced risk-benefit analysis have required?

**Why the answer matters:**

The record shows unusually durable carry-forward of adverse-effect risk evidence but much weaker visible carry-forward of foot-specific modifiability evidence. The expert must decide whether that asymmetry matters clinically.

**Question type:** Standard-of-care / risk-benefit / treatment-strategy.

## Question 8 — Should the therapeutic question have been framed as dose alone, or separately as dose, timing, formulation and coverage?

**Decision period:** 2021–2025, with particular focus on 2022–2024.

**Evidence available at those times:**

- Dyskinesia sensitivity constrained total levodopa exposure.
- Sinemet use was already adjusted according to demand.
- Morning foot symptoms had a reproducible temporal pattern by 2022.

**Proposition for the expert to decide:**

At what earliest point did the contemporaneous evidence make it clinically reasonable to consider redistribution, timing changes, formulation changes, controlled-release coverage, absorption conditions, or other temporal strategies separately from simply “more versus less” total levodopa? Identify the date and the evidence that crossed that threshold.

**Why the answer matters:**

The 2025 CR result demonstrates that timing/formulation could matter without necessarily requiring indiscriminate total-dose escalation. The expert should determine when that became a reasonable question prospectively.

**Question type:** Treatment-strategy / standard-of-care.

## Question 10 — What does the 2025 Sinemet CR response establish, and how far backward may it legitimately reach?

**Decision period:** March 2025.

**Evidence available at that time:**

- Severe left-dominant morning foot pain after a prolonged overnight medication gap.
- The 2025 clinic note records that once Parkinson medication reached the system, the feet felt better from a pain perspective.
- Neurology explicitly considered overnight under-coverage and started Sinemet CR near bedtime.
- The complaint response then recorded **profound relief currently** from the CR regimen.
- Earlier procyclidine withdrawal evidence and later OFF-related pain evidence already existed.

**Proposition for the expert to decide:**

Does the CR response support overnight dopaminergic under-coverage, dystonia, central pain modulation, altered loading, or some combination? Does it strengthen the earlier neurological-modifiability finding without proving that one identical pain generator existed throughout the entire history?

**Why the answer matters:**

This is the strongest later treatment-state experiment in the record. Its significance must be separated from hindsight: it may validate a general modulation proposition while still not proving that CR would necessarily have worked years earlier.

**Question type:** Mechanism / causation / counterfactual.

# 2. Single hinge question

The interpretation of the later pain-management strategy most depends on this:

> **Once the 2017–2018 withdrawal/rechallenge had produced patient-specific evidence that the foot symptom complex itself changed with neurological medication state, what clinical weight should that finding have retained when disabling foot pain recurred in 2022–2025, particularly while pregabalin was being used and dopaminergic treatment was being constrained by dyskinesia and behavioural-risk concerns?**

If the expert concludes that the earlier finding should have remained a durable active premise, then the later management must be evaluated as a **risk-benefit strategy built on two competing patient-specific facts**: dopaminergic treatment caused significant adverse effects, but the foot syndrome had also demonstrated neurological medication sensitivity.

If the expert concludes that the 2017–2018 foot finding was too nonspecific, too confounded, or too clinically weak to retain that force, then the later separation between pregabalin management and dopaminergic restraint becomes easier to justify.

---

# 3. Strongest positive-control comparison

The strongest positive control is the handling of **dyskinesia / behavioural-risk evidence**.

Once patient-specific adverse effects from dopaminergic treatment appeared, they became durable governing premises across years. Higher Neupro exposure produced dyskinesia/chorea and behavioural/impulse-control concerns; later low-dose levodopa / Stalevo experience reinforced dyskinesia sensitivity. Those risks were repeatedly cited in later treatment decisions and again in the 2025 complaint response as reasons for slow and cautious dopaminergic escalation.

By contrast, the earlier patient-specific finding that the foot symptom complex changed rapidly with neurological medication state was clearly documented and locally acted upon, but it had much less visible longitudinal force when the foot problem later recurred.

The expert should therefore be asked:

> **Why did patient-specific adverse-effect evidence remain a durable governing premise across years, while patient-specific foot-modifiability evidence did not retain equally visible longitudinal force, and did that asymmetry have clinical significance for later risk-benefit assessment?**

This comparison does not infer motive. It tests the internal consistency of longitudinal evidence weighting.

# 4. Documentary/reliability questions that should accompany the clinical questions

These are not substitutes for the ranked clinical questions, but they should be put to the expert where relevant.

### A. Retrospective pregabalin rationale

The contemporaneous 2023 record shows pregabalin as a **GP-originated treatment for severe burning / neuropathic-type pain**, later escalated through clinicians before Neurology became centrally involved in the pain pathway.

The 2025 complaint response states that pregabalin was introduced for neuropathic pain **“in part to try to spare further Sinemet increase.”**

The expert should be asked whether that later statement is best understood as:

- an accurate description of the original prescribing rationale;
- a later-acquired management function projected backward;
- or a retrospective reconstruction that compresses two different phases of management.

### B. Conditional neuropathy language

The 2025 clinic letter itself says, **“If there is a degree of peripheral neuropathy here…”** The complaint response then quotes that conditional wording to support the proposition that alcohol had not been treated as the sole explanation.

The expert should therefore distinguish:

- neuropathic-type symptom description;
- possible peripheral neuropathy;
- established peripheral neuropathy;
- and an underlying pain generator that remained unresolved.

Pregabalin use or response must not itself be treated as independent proof of neuropathy.

### C. Complaint-response reconstruction of continuity

The 2025 complaint response accurately preserves some long-standing elements of the record, particularly dopaminergic caution and dyskinesia risk. But it also retrospectively presents a more integrated pain-management story in which:

- Sinemet had been understood “for quite some time” to help pain;
- pregabalin is placed partly inside a levodopa-sparing strategy;
- and the overall course is described as one continuous cautious management architecture.

The expert should be asked which components of that account are independently supported by contemporaneous documentation at the relevant dates and which components are better understood as later synthesis.

# 5. Re-synthesis points for expert attention

The following are the strongest points at which accumulating evidence may have materially changed what was knowable and therefore may have warranted explicit re-synthesis:

1. **October 2017 withdrawal:** feet/gait/tension worsened during planned procyclidine withdrawal.
2. **April 2018 Neurology review:** rapid return of the outside-left-foot / corn-associated phenomenon was documented during the withdrawal period.
3. **2019 recurrent foot referral:** pain and “clawing feet” were again linked in referral language to increased tone and abnormal Parkinsonian gait, while structural findings remained present.
4. **2022 morning pattern:** feet required 20–25 minutes to “warm up,” introducing a reproducible temporal clue after Sinemet had replaced procyclidine.
5. **March 2023 crisis:** severe burning pain persisted despite very high-dose pregabalin, forcing reconsideration of the explanatory model.
6. **Later 2023 OFF model:** pain/shuffling were linked to missed Sinemet and treatment was changed on an OFF-related basis.
7. **2024 dissociation:** pain improvement on pregabalin coexisted with persistent toe curling, separating at least some pain and motor/postural components.
8. **2025 overnight-gap model:** the patient reported severe morning pain after an overnight medication gap and relief once Parkinson medication reached the system.
9. **2025 Sinemet CR response:** controlled-release overnight coverage was followed by reported profound relief.
10. **2025 complaint response:** the clinician retrospectively reconstructed the course and expressly linked pain relief, dyskinesia risk, pregabalin, and Sinemet into one management narrative.

# 6. Required threshold findings before counterfactual analysis

The expert should provide three explicit findings before addressing any counterfactual:

### A. Modifiability threshold

> **What is the earliest date at which the record established, to a clinically meaningful degree, that the foot symptom complex itself was neurologically medication-state modifiable?**

The answer should distinguish broad motor benefit from foot-specific evidence and should explain the significance of the October 2017 withdrawal and April 2018 focal outside-left-foot/corn-associated return.

### B. Re-synthesis threshold

> **What is the earliest later date at which the accumulated contemporaneous evidence made it clinically reasonable to require explicit re-synthesis of the foot-pain model, rather than continued local management of separate components?**

Potential candidate points include 2019 recurrence, the 2022 morning warm-up pattern, the March 2023 severe pain crisis, and the later 2023 OFF-related evidence. The expert should select, reject, or qualify each candidate on clinical grounds.

### C. Timing/formulation/coverage threshold

> **What is the earliest date at which a structured therapeutic test of timing, formulation, absorption conditions or overnight dopaminergic coverage became clinically reasonable, given the known dyskinesia constraint?**

The expert must keep this distinct from a recommendation simply to increase total levodopa dose.

Only after answering A–C should the expert address whether an earlier intervention would probably have changed outcome.

# 7. Counterfactual question — only if the expert first finds an earlier missed re-synthesis threshold

If, and only if, the expert concludes that a re-synthesis or timing/formulation trial should reasonably have occurred earlier, ask:

> **Using only the evidence that existed at the relevant earlier date, is it medically supportable that an earlier structured test of dopaminergic timing, formulation or overnight coverage would probably have reduced the patient’s pain burden, disability, pregabalin exposure, functional deterioration, or duration of suffering? If so, over what time period and with what degree of confidence?**

The expert should be required to identify the earliest date at which that counterfactual becomes supportable and to distinguish probability from mere possibility.
