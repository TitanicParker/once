# Facts

Status labels: **D** documentary; **I** strong inference; **E** expert-dependent; **C** contested.

## Original presentation and anatomy

- **F-001 [D]** On 10 Apr 2017 the GP recorded bilateral foot pain, abnormal gait, mallet toes, onychogryphosis and callus/pressure areas. **CLIN-000018–000039.**
- **F-002 [D]** By 8 Jun the GP was considering Neurology specifically for gait, feet and “muscle tension.” **CLIN-000064–000085.**
- **F-003 [D]** By late Jun marked increased leg tone, abnormal gait, deteriorating dexterity and work failure were documented. **CLIN-000190–000225; CLIN-000847–000884.**
- **F-004 [D]** Hospital examination documented severe Parkinsonism plus pes planus/hammertoes and a foot-drop-like gait considered suggestive of dystonic posture but “NOT FOOT DROP.” **CLIN-001085–001120.**
- **F-005 [D]** Neurology expressly recorded that the relationship between leg symptoms, corns/calluses and burning was “Not clear.” **CLIN-001142–001149.**
- **F-006 [D]** The patient’s historical account distinguished foot pain from leg tension and reported less foot pain after leaving bar work/reducing time on feet. **CLIN-000965–000976.**
- **F-007 [D]** The GP phoned Podiatry on 6 Jul and recorded the case would be prioritised “as Parkinson’s.” **CLIN-000899–000912.**
- **F-008 [D]** The hospital discharge/Podiatry request asked for review of foot issues/outpatient Podiatry but did not frame a defined causal neurological–biomechanical question. **CLIN-000343–000345; CLIN-001225–001229.**

## Focal foot signal

- **F-009 [D]** Left fifth-MTPJ callus/HD recurred repeatedly from Sep 2017 through Mar 2019. **CLIN-002860–002988.**
- **F-010 [D]** On 7 Dec 2018 the patient reported “pain under the left side of my left foot”; Podiatry documented left fifth-MTPJ callus and offloaded that site. **CLIN-002965–002973.**
- **F-011 [D]** Later records again describe an outside-left corn/discomfort, but not every later “outside-left” reference is medically mapped to the exact fifth MTPJ.
- **F-012 [D]** The record also contains materially different foot-pain phenotypes: bilateral pain, burning feet/lower legs, circumferential/coronal pain, morning soreness and OFF-associated leg/foot symptoms.

## Initial management and external advice

- **F-013 [D]** July 2017 Neurology deliberately planned to avoid levodopa/dopamine agonists as long as possible because of very young onset, rapid dyskinesia risk and ICD/punding risk. **CLIN-001166–001180.**
- **F-014 [D]** Galway explicitly asked Prof Healy whether its levodopa-deferral position should change: “unless you'd advise otherwise?” **CLIN-001720–001730.**
- **F-015 [D]** Healy wrote that the patient “probably has Park II,” recommended PARK2 testing and said PARK II patients can manage for long periods on relatively low-dose dopamine replacement but become dyskinetic if over-used. **CLIN-001741–001760.**
- **F-016 [D]** Healy did not order immediate levodopa.
- **F-017 [D]** In 2018 Neurology still described PARK2/PRKN as what “we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause,” while stating testing might not change current management. **CLIN-001950–001957.**

## Treatment-state evidence

- **F-018 [D]** Procyclidine produced dramatic global functional improvement; during withdrawal the patient contemporaneously reported increasing tension, restless feet and gait falling apart while eyesight improved. **CLIN-001770–001813.**
- **F-019 [D]** This establishes treatment-state sensitivity involving feet/gait/tension; it does not establish the mechanism of a corn or prove levodopa would have the same effect.
- **F-020 [D]** By 2019 Neurology expressly described treatment as a “tight rope” between stiffness/discomfort and impulsivity/compulsions/chorea-dyskinesia and preferred delaying levodopa as long as possible. **CLIN-002147–002166.**
- **F-021 [D]** In 2020 a foot-throw/foot-drop-like movement was classified as “actually parkinsonian” because power was normal. **CLIN-002282–002290.**

## Levodopa transition

- **F-022 [D]** Sinemet first began in Sep 2021 at 62.5 mg BD because the patient felt undertreated; Kemadrin was then removed for ocular-pressure concerns and Sinemet increased to QID. **CLIN-002330–002354.**
- **F-023 [D]** The 2021 note expressly continued a Sinemet-sparing/minimal-dose principle. **CLIN-002351–002354.**
- **F-024 [I]** No structured 2021 foot-by-dose assessment is identified for onset, wearing-off, overnight state, first steps, focal lateral-left pain, clawing/curling or gait/loading.

## 2022–2024 mixed evidence

- **F-025 [D]** In 2022 the feet took 20–25 minutes to “warm up” most mornings, were sore and had “coronal pain.”
- **F-026 [D]** In Mar 2023 the GP reported severe bilateral burning feet/lower legs, “death wish,” with pregabalin escalated from 25 mg BD through various doctors and only just controlling symptoms. **CLIN-002637–002641.**
- **F-027 [D]** A Parkinson nurse recorded that pregabalin had been commenced by the GP. **CLIN-002681–002684.**
- **F-028 [D]** The same nurse recorded the patient’s report of ten days off Parkinson medication with no perceived Parkinsonian difference. **CLIN-002681–002684.**
- **F-029 [D]** Later 2023 nursing evidence linked severe foot pain with reduced mobility/shuffling and worse mornings/later when Sinemet had not been taken; the nurse assessed leg pain/shuffling as OFF-related and Stalevo was started. **CLIN-002574–002582.**
- **F-030 [D]** Neurology separately interpreted usual evening protective stance/lack movement/short steps as typical OFF Parkinson’s and considered insufficient levodopa while recognising dyskinesia risk. **CLIN-002449–002474.**
- **F-031 [D]** Lyrica substantially reduced foot pain by 2023–24, but toe curling persisted. **CLIN-002447–002448; CLIN-002698–002710.**

## 2025 balancing and complaint

- **F-032 [D]** By 2025 the patient had self-reduced Lyrica from 600 mg/day to 50 mg TDS; Neurology states it had not suggested reduction. **CLIN-002767–002800.**
- **F-033 [D]** The 2025 morning phenotype included “murder in the feet,” first 150 steps “torture,” mainly left, with relief once Parkinson medication reached the system. **CLIN-002777–002780.**
- **F-034 [D]** Neurology said more Sinemet would probably provide some further pain relief but likely worsen dyskinesia; it considered increasing Lyrica partly to avoid increasing Parkinson medication, then prescribed nighttime Sinemet CR. **CLIN-002787–002800.**
- **F-035 [D]** The 2025 complaint response expressly defended the original levodopa-sparing strategy and described CR as providing “profound relief currently.” **CLIN-003260–003270.**
- **F-036 [D]** The complaint response stated Lyrica had been “introduced… in part to try to spare further Sinemet increase.” **CLIN-003265.**
- **F-037 [D]** Contemporaneous initiation evidence instead identifies the GP as the initiator for severe foot/leg pain; no identified contemporaneous source establishes Sinemet-sparing as the original prescribing purpose. **CLIN-002637–002641; CLIN-002681–002684.**
- **F-038 [I]** The 2025 “introduced…to spare Sinemet” wording is therefore a retrospective purpose attribution whose original provenance is not established by the available record.

## Genetics

- **F-039 [D]** In Feb 2026 two pathogenic Parkin variants were confirmed; Parkinsonism and dystonia were attributed to compound heterozygous PRKN mutations. **CLIN-003220–003228.**
- **F-040 [D]** The 2026 result confirms the earlier PARK2 branch but cannot establish what should have been known or done in 2017.
