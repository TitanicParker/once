# Management Plan as a Longitudinal Object

## 1. Original policy

The first explicit policy appears in July 2017. Neurology planned “the greatest of care” with usual dopamine agonists/levodopa because disease onset may have been in the mid-20s; it was likely to “avoid these for as long as possible” because of rapid dyskinesia and impulse-control/punding risk. **CLIN-001166–001180.**

This was a prospective policy, not merely absence of prescription. It sat beside:
- alternative symptomatic treatment (clonazepam, rasagiline, procyclidine);
- a structural/local Podiatry pathway;
- an unresolved causal formulation: “Not clear.”

## 2. After Healy

Galway asked whether holding back should change. Healy said probable PARK2 patients may manage for long periods on relatively low-dose dopamine replacement but become dyskinetic if over-used. No immediate levodopa was ordered. The Galway deferral policy remained operative.

**Classification:** CONTINUATION OF PRIOR POLICY after external advice.

## 3. After procyclidine withdrawal

Withdrawal supplied strong treatment-state evidence involving feet/gait/tension. Local response: reintroduction/re-escalation because benefit outweighed visual toxicity at tolerable doses. No documented historical reclassification of the feet/Podiatry relationship is identified.

**Classification:** LOCAL MANAGEMENT CHANGE; NO IDENTIFIED GLOBAL REASSESSMENT.

## 4. 2018–2019

PARK2 remained suspected but genetics was described as unlikely to alter current management. Dopamine agonist exposure generated real chorea/behavioural concerns. By 2019 Neurology expressly preferred to leave levodopa “as long as is humanly possible.”

**Classification:** EXPLICIT CONTINUATION OF PRIOR POLICY, now with patient-specific toxicity evidence.

## 5. 2020 Parkinsonian foot findings

One abnormal foot movement was now expressly neurological. No note is identified that reconstructs the historic pain/structural partition in consequence.

**Classification:** INTEGRATING EVIDENCE; NO IDENTIFIED GLOBAL REASSESSMENT.

## 6. 2021 Sinemet

Literal levodopa non-use ends. Sinemet starts for undertreatment and replaces Kemadrin partly because of ocular pressure. Yet the note continues a Sinemet-sparing/minimal-dose principle.

The key design issue is not “why no levodopa?” but whether the new regimen was measured against the already-known treatment-sensitive lower-limb phenotype. No structured foot-by-dose map is identified.

**Classification:** MAJOR LOCAL/BROADER TREATMENT CHANGE; MODIFIED CONTINUATION OF RISK POLICY; NO DOCUMENTED HISTORICAL REPARTITION.

## 7. 2022–2024

Morning foot warm-up, 2023 OFF/undertreatment, Stalevo, pregabalin and pain/posture dissociation generate a mixed model. Neurology increasingly recognises neurological modulation and separate pain components. Treatment is repeatedly adjusted.

**Classification:** PARTIAL CLINICAL INTEGRATION + COMPONENT-SPECIFIC MANAGEMENT.

## 8. 2025

The overnight/first-step phenotype is explicitly mapped. Neurology says more Sinemet would likely relieve some pain but increase dyskinesia; Lyrica increase is considered to avoid Parkinson-medication increase; nighttime CR is then prescribed and later said to provide profound relief.

The complaint response does not state that the original partition was wrong. Instead it defends the historical levodopa-sparing strategy and preserves a dual model: pain may arise from another cause while Parkinson/OFF state magnifies experience.

**Classification:** TARGETED LOCAL CHANGE + RETROSPECTIVE DEFENCE OF PRIOR POLICY.

## 9. 2026 PRKN

PRKN confirmation materially changes diagnostic certainty and confirms Parkinsonism/dystonia. It does not establish the historic pain generator or prove earlier treatment error. No post-genetic historical foot reanalysis by the original service is identified in this record.

## Central conclusion

The literal policy “do not use levodopa” ended in 2021. A higher-order architecture remained recognisable through the complaint:
1. minimise dopamine exposure in a very young dyskinesia-sensitive patient;
2. use alternative symptom-control routes where possible;
3. allow primary foot pain to remain structurally/peripherally attributable even when Parkinson state modifies its expression.

The record shows repeated **local updating**. It does not identify a comparably explicit **global reopening** of the original 2017 structural–neurological relationship.

That is a documentary finding about architecture, not a finding that the architecture was clinically wrong.
