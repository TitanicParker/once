# RECORD FINDINGS REGISTER

**Purpose:**  
This document captures the outputs produced by applying `HOW-TO-READ-COMPLETE-RECORD.md` to `complete-record.md`.

It is not itself a clinical opinion, legal opinion, complaint, or advocacy document. Its purpose is to separate:

- facts within the record;
- facts about the record;
- interpretive consequences;
- unresolved propositions;
- decision points; and
- questions requiring external clinical or other expert judgment.

The register should remain auditable. Each finding should be traceable back to the underlying record and should distinguish contemporaneous evidence from later reconstruction.

---

## 1. How to Use This Register

For each material issue:

1. Identify the **case fact**: what happened, was reported, observed, prescribed, tested, referred, or documented.
2. Identify any **record fact**: a fact about timing, framing, authorship, referral wording, repetition, inheritance, observation conditions, retrospective reconstruction, or evidential development.
3. State the **interpretive consequence**: what the record fact changes about what the case fact can safely prove.
4. Record any **counterevidence or competing evidence**.
5. State what remains **unresolved**.
6. Identify any **decision point** at which the new evidence could have altered assessment, investigation, referral, treatment, or communication.
7. Where appropriate, formulate an **expert question** rather than reaching an expert conclusion.

Do not convert absence of documentation into proof that something did not occur.

Do not treat a later explanation as proof that the same rationale existed contemporaneously.

Do not treat treatment response alone as proof of mechanism.

Do not collapse structural pathology, neurological motor phenomena, pain generation, pain modulation, and functional consequences into a single proposition.

---

## 2. Status Vocabulary

Use one of the following labels where useful:

- **Established documentary fact** — directly supported by the record.
- **Supported proposition** — supported by multiple or converging entries but still interpretive.
- **Documentary tension** — two or more record elements cannot safely be reduced to one simple account.
- **Retrospective reconstruction** — a later account attributes meaning, rationale, knowledge, or continuity to an earlier period.
- **Inherited framing** — a later entry repeats or adopts an earlier classification without clearly adding new evidence.
- **Observation-window limitation** — the observation may be accurate but may not represent the patient's state across the day, medication cycle, or symptom cycle.
- **Non-monotonic treatment response** — response varies over time and cannot safely be summarized as simply effective or ineffective.
- **Unresolved proposition** — the record leaves the issue open.
- **Expert question** — the record can identify the question but cannot answer it without external expertise.

# Governing Rule

> The purpose of this register is not to produce a cleaner story than the source record supports. It is to preserve the distinction between what happened, what was documented, what the record itself reveals about that documentation, what can reasonably be inferred, and what remains for expert judgment.

A finding should be capable of being challenged at each level:

**source → case fact → record fact → interpretive consequence → unresolved proposition → expert question.**

If any link cannot be shown, the conclusion should be weakened accordingly.
