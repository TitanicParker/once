# REFERRAL / SPECIALIST-ESCALATION REGISTER

## Purpose

This register captures the referral and escalation history relevant to the clinical argument.

The central distinction is not simply **whether referrals occurred**, but:

> **What question was actually referred, to whom, and what clinically important question is not shown in the surviving record as having been referred for independent adjudication?**

This is not a finding that any referral was inadequate. It is a structured record of the documented referral architecture and the comments that arise from it.

## R01 — July 2017 — Inpatient Physiotherapy and Occupational Therapy

### Referrer
Neurology / admitting team.

### Receiving service
Physiotherapy and Occupational Therapy.

### Referral / involvement documented
The discharge material records Physiotherapy and Occupational Therapy under **“OTHER SPECIALTIES CONSULTED.”**

### Question actually referred
Functional assessment, rehabilitation, mobility, dexterity and practical consequences of the new neurological diagnosis.

### Comment
These were appropriate functional and rehabilitative inputs.

They show that Neurology recognised from the outset that the presentation had major functional consequences.

They were not, however, diagnostic referrals directed to the central movement-disorders question. There is no surviving wording showing that Physio or OT were asked to determine whether:

- toe clawing;
- abnormal foot posture;
- corns/calluses;
- recurrent foot pain; or
- altered loading

formed part of a dystonic or Parkinsonian lower-limb phenotype.

### Classification
**Completed / operative multidisciplinary input**

## R02 — July 2017 — Podiatry / Chiropody

### Referrer
Neurology / hospital team, with GP follow-through.

### Receiving service
Podiatry / Chiropody.

### Referral documented
The discharge material states:

> “Podiatry referral has been sent to review issues with feet reported by the patient.”

The fuller neurological plan also asks for outpatient Chiropody/Podiatry and records that the SHO had already tried to arrange it in hospital.

### Question actually referred
Assessment and management of the patient's foot problems.

### Important question not shown as referred
The surviving referral wording does not demonstrate that Podiatry was asked to adjudicate:

- whether dystonia was causing or contributing to toe clawing;
- whether abnormal Parkinsonian tone altered loading;
- whether recurrent lateral-foot lesions were downstream of abnormal posture;
- whether neurological medication state changed the foot manifestations;
- or how the structural and neurological findings should be integrated.

### Comment
This is an important distinction.

At the same time that Neurology had documented a **“strikingly abnormal”** gait and a dystonic-looking lower-limb posture, the feet were also directed into a Podiatry pathway.

That does not make the Podiatry referral inappropriate. Genuine structural pathology was documented and required care.

The narrower documentary point is:

> **The feet were referred for assessment/treatment, but the unresolved causal relationship between the foot pathology and the neurological phenotype is not demonstrably shown as the question referred for adjudication.**

### Classification
**Referral sent / outpatient pathway requested**

## R06 — 2017 — Prof Daniel Healy / Beaumont

### Referrer
Dr Thomas Monaghan.

### Receiving specialist
Prof Daniel G. Healy, Consultant Neurologist, Beaumont Hospital.

### Referral documented
The correspondence sought:

- advice about appropriate genetic testing;
- whether genetic testing was worthwhile;
- genetic laboratory contacts;
- possible review in Dublin;
- a **“once off opinion”**;
- and advice about whether levodopa should continue to be held back.

A clinical video had also been prepared for possible Beaumont second-opinion use.

### Question actually referred
Broadly:

> **What genetic disorder is this? What testing should be done? Would a specialist Dublin opinion help? Should the current levodopa-restraint strategy continue?**

### Specialist response
Prof Healy then saw the patient and recorded:

- that the patient probably had **PARK II**;
- that PARK2 should be tested first;
- dramatic motor improvement on procyclidine and rasagiline;
- the possibility of long periods on relatively low doses of dopamine replacement;
- and the risk of becoming markedly dyskinetic if over-treated.

He remained willing to review again if required.

### Important question not shown as referred
The surviving referral does not demonstrably ask:

> **How should the longstanding foot pain, toe clawing, dystonic posture, structural foot pathology and medication-state responsiveness be integrated into this unusually young PARK2/PRKN-like movement disorder?**

### Comment
This is the major external specialist escalation in the record and should not be minimised.

But its **scope** matters.

The visible questions concerned genetics, PARK2, testing, second opinion and levodopa strategy.

Accordingly, Healy's relative silence about the foot syndrome cannot safely be converted into an expert conclusion that the feet were unrelated to the movement disorder if that question was not itself clearly referred.

### Classification
**Completed external specialist review**

# Referral pattern

The documentary record does **not** support a simple proposition that the neurologist was reluctant to refer or failed to use multidisciplinary care.

The record shows use of:

- Physiotherapy;
- Occupational Therapy;
- Podiatry;
- Occupational Health;
- Parkinson's specialist nursing;
- Prof Healy / Beaumont;
- potential genetic testing;
- potential clinical-trial centres;
- possible DBS;
- and discussion of chronic-pain expertise.

The stronger referral question is therefore:

> **What questions were referred, and what questions were not?**

The visible architecture can be summarised as:

| Domain | Service / pathway | Question visibly allocated |
|---|---|---|
| Function | Physiotherapy / OT | Mobility, dexterity, rehabilitation, function |
| Structural feet | Podiatry | Review / treatment of foot problems |
| Work capacity | Occupational Health | Fitness for work / redeployment |
| Parkinson's treatment support | Parkinson's ANP | Medication management and follow-up |
| Genetics / PARK2 / treatment strategy | Prof Healy | Genetics, PARK2, testing, second opinion, levodopa restraint |
| Future research | Trial centres | Potential clinical-trial participation |
| Advanced therapy | DBS pathway | Possible future DBS assessment |
| Pain | Chronic-pain specialist | Discussed, but completed referral not demonstrated |

## Central unresolved referral question

What is not demonstrated in the surviving referral record is a completed referral squarely framed as:

> **How should the longstanding foot pain, toe clawing, dystonic posture, structural foot pathology and medication-state responsiveness be integrated into this unusually young PRKN/PARK2-like movement disorder?**

After Prof Healy's one-off assessment, the surviving record also does not demonstrate a further completed external movement-disorders referral during 2017–2025 despite later emergence of:

- dopamine-agonist dyskinesia;
- behavioural treatment effects;
- procyclidine toxicity;
- levodopa sensitivity;
- recurrent severe foot pain;
- OFF-related pain and shuffling;
- persistent clawing despite analgesic improvement;
- and the eventual overnight-coverage problem.

That does not establish that such referral was required.

It identifies the question for independent expert adjudication:

> **At what point, if any, did the accumulating phenotype and treatment complexity make renewed movement-disorders specialist assessment or co-management clinically indicated?**
