# Tension Register

## Status and method

This file is the **tension layer** between the documentary facts and any later propositions or expert questions.

It is derived from [`FACTS.md`](FACTS.md), the operative corrections in [`FACTS-AUDIT-CORRECTIONS.md`](FACTS-AUDIT-CORRECTIONS.md), and ultimately [`complete-record.md`](complete-record.md).

A tension is not an allegation and is not a conclusion. It exists where two or more established documentary positions require integration, reconciliation, temporal re-weighting, or specialist classification.

### Rules

- No tension is resolved here.
- Structural and neurological explanations are not treated as mutually exclusive unless the evidence requires it.
- Treatment response does not establish mechanism.
- Patient attribution and clinician attribution are recorded as attribution, not objective causation.
- Later evidence is not back-projected into earlier knowledge.
- Documentary silence is not proof that thought, discussion, examination or integration did not occur.
- Adverse evidence travels with the tension it qualifies.
- The relevant question is often not whether an earlier position was reasonable when first adopted, but whether its adequacy changed as the evidential state changed.

# T-001 — Objective structural pathology ↔ neurological state dependence

### Facts engaged

F-002, F-005, F-015, F-019–F-022, F-046, F-049, F-058–F-059, F-086–F-090, F-113, F-115, F-168–F-171, F-194, F-196–F-198.

### Documentary position A — structural/peripheral evidence

The record contains positive structural findings: mallet/hammertoes, pes planus, recurrent callus/HD, focal fifth-MTPJ pathology, local pain and repeated mechanical treatment/offloading. The Podiatry pathway therefore cannot be treated as if it addressed imaginary or purely neurological pathology.

### Documentary position B — neurological evidence

The same longitudinal record contains marked tone, clawing, dystonic-appearing posture, abnormal gait/leg throw, and medication-state changes involving gait, tension and the feet. In 2020 the foot/leg throw was explicitly classified as Parkinsonian.

### Why a tension exists

Both strands are independently documented. A model in which one automatically invalidates the other does not fit the factual field. The unresolved issue is their relationship: independent coexistence, neurological amplification, abnormal loading, dystonic contribution, or some combination.

### Strongest benign reconciliation

The patient had genuine podiatric disease alongside a neurological movement disorder; each was appropriately treated within its own domain, and neurological treatment could alter mobility or pain experience without causing the structural lesion.

### Alternative reconciliation

Neurological tone, posture, clawing or gait may have contributed to loading, recurrence or symptom expression at structurally vulnerable sites while genuine local pathology remained present.

### Evidential boundary

The documents do not establish that dystonia caused any particular corn, callus, fifth-MTPJ lesion or pain episode.

### Temporal state

Visible from entry in 2017 as coexistence and explicit uncertainty. It becomes more clinically consequential after neurological treatment-state effects involving the feet are documented.

### Status

**OPEN — requires specialist integration.**

# T-002 — Defensible 2017 uncertainty ↔ accumulating later evidence

### Facts engaged

F-046, F-054–F-059, F-064, F-081, F-086–F-097, F-103, F-111, F-115, F-125–F-128, F-138–F-140, F-146, F-153–F-160, F-195, F-197–F-204.

### Documentary position A — initial uncertainty

In July 2017 Neurology expressly recorded that the relationship between the leg/foot symptoms, corns/calluses, burning and possible peripheral neuropathy was `Not clear`. The patient himself historically distinguished foot pain from leg tension, and genuine structural pathology existed.

### Documentary position B — later accumulation

The subsequent record adds procyclidine withdrawal effects involving restless feet/gait/tension; dystonic elements and Parkinsonian classification of the leg throw; morning/OFF patterns; pregabalin-responsive pain with persistent toe curling; later levodopa-state effects; and substantial reported benefit after overnight Sinemet CR.

### Why a tension exists

A position can be reasonable at time A yet require reconsideration after facts B, C and D accumulate. The issue is not whether July 2017 uncertainty was impermissible; it is whether, and when, the evidential state changed enough that the original unresolved relationship required explicit re-synthesis.

### Strongest benign reconciliation

The later observations remained compatible with a stable mixed model: structural/peripheral pain with Parkinsonian modulation. Clinicians could therefore preserve the original distinction while adjusting treatment locally.

### Alternative reconciliation

Repeated cross-domain evidence progressively weakened the adequacy of leaving the relationship merely unresolved and created a point at which explicit integrated reassessment became clinically indicated.

### Evidential boundary

The documents alone cannot define the clinical threshold at which reassessment became required.

### Temporal state

Begins as legitimate uncertainty in July 2017. October 2017 is the first major treatment-state addition. Later evidence accumulates through 2020, 2022–2025.

### Status

**OPEN — threshold and timing require expert determination.**

# T-003 — 2023 ten-day medication cessation ↔ contemporaneous OFF/Stalevo observations

### Facts engaged

F-130–F-140, F-202.

### Documentary position A — reported absence of perceived medication effect

In March 2023 the patient reported stopping Parkinson medication for ten days and perceiving no Parkinsonian difference while severe bilateral foot/leg pain remained a dominant problem.

### Documentary position B — documented OFF phenomena

Other 2023 nursing/neurology material records reduced mobility, shuffling and leg pain as OFF/undertreatment phenomena, with Stalevo/levodopa adjustment and functional benefit associated with medication state.

### Why a tension exists

These accounts cannot responsibly be collapsed into a single unqualified statement that Parkinson medication either did or did not affect the foot/lower-limb syndrome.

### Strongest benign reconciliation

Different symptom components, times of day, medication regimens or the dominance of severe pain may explain why the ten-day cessation produced no perceived difference while other encounters captured OFF-related motor/pain phenomena.

### Alternative reconciliation

The causal interpretation of medication response may have varied between encounters, and some apparent effects may have been context- or state-dependent rather than stable.

### Evidential boundary

There is no surviving blinded or standardised OFF/ON assessment of the painful feet capable of resolving the contradiction.

### Temporal state

Acute tension within 2023; later 2025 medication-timing evidence must be considered without erasing the adverse 2023 report.

### Status

**OPEN — requires specialist reconciliation.**

# T-004 — Pregabalin-responsive pain ↔ persistent toe curling and motor phenomena

### Facts engaged

F-132–F-135, F-141, F-144–F-147, F-203.

### Documentary position A — analgesic response

Pregabalin/Lyrica was used for severe burning bilateral foot/lower-leg pain, and by 2024 the record states that it removed much of the foot pain, particularly on the right.

### Documentary position B — persistent motor/postural phenomenon

Toe curling persisted and could be provoked by activity even when pain had materially improved. Mild dyskinesia also remained relevant to treatment decisions.

### Why a tension exists

The foot syndrome does not behave as one indivisible variable. Pain and toe posture can change differently under treatment.

### Strongest benign reconciliation

Pregabalin treated a neuropathic or other pain component while an independent dystonic/Parkinsonian or mechanical toe-posturing phenomenon persisted.

### Alternative reconciliation

Pain and motor phenomena were interacting components of a mixed syndrome but were pharmacologically separable.

### Evidential boundary

Pregabalin response does not identify the pain generator, and persistent toe curling does not itself classify the movement phenomenology.

### Temporal state

Most clearly visible in 2024.

### Status

**OPEN — symptom decomposition requires specialist classification.**

# T-005 — Dopaminergic/anticholinergic benefit ↔ treatment toxicity and therapeutic constraint

### Facts engaged

F-065–F-069, F-081–F-095, F-100–F-107, F-116–F-121, F-147, F-155–F-161, F-181–F-182.

### Documentary position A — repeated benefit/sensitivity

Procyclidine/rasagiline produced dramatic motor improvement; higher-dose Neupro could produce excellent motor states; levodopa later affected mobility/pain and Sinemet CR was associated with major reported benefit.

### Documentary position B — repeated toxicity/risk

Procyclidine produced visual/ocular concerns; higher-dose Neupro produced chorea/dyskinesia and impulse/compulsive concerns; later levodopa produced dyskinesia even at relatively limited exposure. Caution about dyskinesia and behavioural toxicity was documented from 2017 onward.

### Why a tension exists

The record supports both responsiveness and a genuinely narrow therapeutic window. The clinical issue cannot be reduced either to `more dopamine should have been given` or to `toxicity ended the inquiry`.

### Strongest benign reconciliation

Longstanding caution was clinically justified and later adverse effects vindicated the risk model; symptom control therefore required conservative dosing and non-dopaminergic adjuncts.

### Alternative reconciliation

Precisely because the therapeutic window was narrow, systematic symptom-specific testing and allocation of limited dopaminergic exposure may have become more important as disabling foot symptoms accumulated.

### Evidential boundary

The documentary record cannot establish the optimal historical dose, formulation or balance of long-term risks and symptomatic benefit.

### Temporal state

Risk architecture is explicit from 2017 and supported by observed toxicity from 2018 onward; the benefit-risk weighting becomes increasingly important as foot symptoms become disabling.

### Status

**OPEN — benefit-risk proportionality requires expert assessment.**

# T-006 — Patient separation of pain/tension ↔ clinician classification of some foot/leg states as neurological

### Facts engaged

F-033–F-034, F-058–F-059, F-096, F-115, F-136–F-140, F-154–F-160, F-163.

### Documentary position A — patient phenomenology

The patient historically distinguished foot pain from leg tension and in 2023 expressly believed the severe bilateral pain was not Parkinson-related.

### Documentary position B — clinician classification

Neurology classified some abnormal leg/foot movement as Parkinsonian, interpreted some later pain/shuffling states as OFF-related, and recognised that levodopa could improve the patient's pain experience while maintaining that the underlying generator might remain peripheral.

### Why a tension exists

`Cause of lesion`, `cause of pain`, `motor contributor`, and `state-dependent amplifier` are different questions. Patient and clinician statements may appear contradictory because they are addressing different levels of causation.

### Strongest benign reconciliation

The patient correctly perceived a distinct structural/pain syndrome while clinicians correctly identified Parkinsonian modulation of movement and pain experience.

### Alternative reconciliation

A coarse separation between `Parkinson's` and `foot pain` may have obscured a mixed model in which some foot manifestations were neurologically generated or mechanically amplified by dystonia.

### Evidential boundary

Neither patient attribution nor clinician attribution proves mechanism.

### Temporal state

Present from the 2017 history and becomes sharper as neurological classifications and medication-state effects accumulate.

### Status

**OPEN — causal levels require explicit expert separation.**

# T-007 — Podiatry deformity/pathology documentation ↔ internally non-uniform risk classification

### Facts engaged

F-005, F-049, F-165–F-174, F-196, plus operative F-206 and AC-002 in `FACTS-AUDIT-CORRECTIONS.md`.

### Documentary position A — positive pathology/deformity record

The longitudinal record contains mallet/hammertoes, pes planus, recurrent fifth-MTPJ callus/HD, clawing references, repeated local treatment and an older layered referral carrying a `Medium Risk - Foot deformities` tick.

### Documentary position B — later low-risk selections

The 2019 Podiatry re-referral visibly records `No deformity`, `No neuropathy` and `No PAD` as low-risk selections while the same referral separately refers to callus, gait abnormality, clawing feet and pain.

### Why a tension exists

The Podiatry documentary stream is not internally uniform. Risk-form selections and descriptive clinical content do not map neatly onto one another across time.

### Strongest benign reconciliation

The risk-form categories may reflect a specific service-risk definition rather than a literal assertion that no toe/foot deformity existed; documentation practices may also have changed between forms.

### Alternative reconciliation

The inconsistency may reflect fragmented or imprecise classification of the foot phenotype, limiting the reliability of categorical labels when read without the descriptive record.

### Evidential boundary

The documents alone do not establish the intended technical meaning of each Podiatry risk checkbox or why selections changed.

### Temporal state

The contrast is between the older layered referral and the 2019 re-referral.

### Status

**OPEN — documentary/clinical significance requires cautious interpretation.**

# T-008 — Early PARK2/PRKN suspicion ↔ late genetic confirmation

### Facts engaged

F-057, F-079–F-083, F-097, F-175–F-178, F-205.

### Documentary position A — early suspicion without confirmation

Genetic Parkinsonism and specifically PARK2/PRKN were considered in 2017; Healy wrote that the patient probably had Park II and recommended testing. Testing was later deferred on the stated basis that it was not expected to change immediate management.

### Documentary position B — later causal certainty

In February 2026 two pathogenic Parkin variants were confirmed and the consultant attributed the Parkinsonism and dystonia to compound-heterozygous PRKN mutations.

### Why a tension exists

The later result validates the earlier suspicion and materially changes etiological certainty, but it was not knowledge available to earlier clinicians. The unresolved question is not hindsight correctness; it is the potential clinical significance of earlier confirmation when the phenotype already included dystonic features.

### Strongest benign reconciliation

The suspected genetic subtype was already incorporated into clinical thinking, and earlier confirmation would not necessarily have changed symptomatic management.

### Alternative reconciliation

Earlier confirmation could have altered the weight given to dystonia, phenotype-specific expectations, treatment sensitivity or the need to integrate foot manifestations into the neurological formulation.

### Evidential boundary

The documents cannot establish the counterfactual effect of earlier genetic testing on treatment or outcome.

### Temporal state

Suspicion from 2017–2018; certainty only in 2026.

### Status

**OPEN — historical management relevance requires expert evidence.**

# T-009 — Stable therapeutic caution ↔ changing evidential state

### Facts engaged

F-065–F-069, F-094–F-097, F-100–F-107, F-120–F-121, F-125–F-128, F-138–F-140, F-147, F-153–F-161, F-181–F-183, F-201–F-204.

### Documentary position A — continuity of rationale

The rationale for limiting levodopa/dopaminergic exposure was contemporaneously documented from 2017 and remained broadly stable: young age, dyskinesia risk, impulse-control risk and later demonstrated treatment toxicity.

### Documentary position B — changing symptom evidence

The evidential state did not remain static. Later years added medication-sensitive gait/foot phenomena, OFF descriptions, severe disabling pain, morning timing patterns and substantial reported benefit after nocturnal Sinemet CR.

### Why a tension exists

A valid risk rationale may remain valid while the weight assigned to benefit changes. The question is whether the same therapeutic weighting remained proportionate as the symptom evidence evolved.

### Strongest benign reconciliation

The observed toxicity meant that conservative treatment remained appropriate throughout, and later benefit was pursued through careful low-dose/formulation changes rather than aggressive escalation.

### Alternative reconciliation

Accumulating evidence of disabling treatment-responsive symptoms may at some point have required a more explicit reassessment of how the narrow therapeutic window was being used, even if the underlying risks remained unchanged.

### Evidential boundary

The documents cannot themselves determine the appropriate benefit-risk threshold at each date.

### Temporal state

The risk rationale begins in 2017; the counterweight of symptom-specific evidence increases progressively, particularly from 2022–2025.

### Status

**OPEN — proportionality over time requires expert determination.**

# Cross-tension observations

These observations do not resolve the tensions. They identify relationships that later proposition drafting must preserve.

1. **Mixed pathology is the default evidential possibility.** T-001, T-004, T-006 and T-007 make a binary structural-versus-neurological framing unsafe.
2. **Generation and modulation must remain separate.** Evidence that neurological state changes pain or foot function is stronger than evidence that neurological disease generated every structural lesion.
3. **Time changes evidential weight.** T-002 and T-009 require later decisions to be judged against the information then accumulated rather than against the July 2017 state alone.
4. **Adverse evidence is load-bearing.** T-003, T-005 and T-007 must travel with any later proposition based on treatment responsiveness, deformity or therapeutic restraint.
5. **2026 genetics changes etiological certainty but not historical knowledge.** T-008 must not be used to back-project certainty.
6. **The surviving record can show an undocumented synthesis without proving an absent synthesis.** Any later proposition concerning integration must retain that distinction.

No proposition is established by this file.

The next layer should ask, for each tension or combination of tensions:

> **What clinically material proposition would an appropriately qualified independent expert need to determine in order to resolve this tension?**

Several tensions may converge on one proposition. A proposition should not be created merely to preserve a one-to-one numbering scheme.

Only after the proposition layer is stable should neutral expert questions be drafted.
