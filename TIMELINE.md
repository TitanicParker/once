# Clinical Timeline

**Scope:** clinical only, from the presenting history and 2017 referral through the February 2026 genetic confirmation.

**Control:** this is a chronological extraction from `complete-record.md`, not a second argument. Where the source permits, medication information is separated into **Prescription / plan** (what was ordered or recommended) and **Medication / response** (what was actually taken, stopped, restarted, tolerated or reported to help/harm). A prescription does not prove ingestion, and a treatment response does not by itself establish mechanism.

## 2017

- **10 Apr — original foot presentation.** Bilateral foot pain, abnormal gait, marked mallet toes, onychogryphosis and callus/pressure areas; Podiatry planned. **CLIN-000018–000039.**
- **8–29 Jun — problem becomes neurological.** GP links gait, feet and “muscle tension”; initiation difficulty, marked leg tone, brisk reflexes, deteriorating dexterity and work failure. **CLIN-000064–000085; CLIN-000190–000225; CLIN-000847–000884.**
- **3–11 Jul — hospital neurological phenotype.** Markedly increased tone, striking gait, foot-drop-like dystonic posture expressly recorded as **“NOT FOOT DROP,”** rigidity/bradykinesia, pes planus and hammertoes. Corns/calluses, burning and foot pain are also recorded; the relationship is **“Not clear.”** **CLIN-001085–001149.**
- **6 Jul — GP re-contextualises Podiatry “as Parkinson’s.”** **CLIN-000899–000912.**
- **Jul — Prescription / plan.** Clonazepam 0.25 mg nocte for two weeks; rasagiline 1 mg OD; procyclidine 2.5 mg TDS with staged titration; CoQ10. The longer neurology plan proposes rasagiline first, then procyclidine/Kemadrin, while deliberately avoiding conventional levodopa/dopamine-agonist treatment for as long as possible because of young age and anticipated dyskinesia/impulse-control risk. Amantadine is retained as a future option. **CLIN-000343–000365; CLIN-001166–001223.**
- **13 Jul — DaTSCAN.** Very young bilateral Parkinsonism, gross bradykinesia, “walk is almost dystonically unusual,” autosomal-recessive Parkinsonism suspected. **CLIN-001389–001408.**
- **2017 Healy request / 12 Sep response.** Galway asks whether holding back levodopa should change. Healy says the patient “probably has Park II,” recommends PARK2 testing and notes that PARK II patients may manage for long periods with relatively low doses of dopamine replacement but become dyskinetic if over-used. **CLIN-001720–001760.**
- **Sep onward — recurrent left fifth-MTPJ pathology.** First exact site documented 14 Sep; repeated through Mar 2019. **CLIN-002860–002988.**
- **Oct — Medication / response: procyclidine natural experiment.** A planned Kemadrin/procyclidine break is undertaken because of visual effects. The contemporaneous account records major prior motor/function improvement, then increasing tension, restless feet and deterioration of gait during withdrawal; lower dosing is subsequently insufficient and treatment is re-escalated. **CLIN-001770–001813.**

## 2018

- **24 Apr — Medication / current use.** Azilect 1 mg daily; procyclidine 2.5 mg QDS (variable); CoQ10 about 200 mg/day; folate 5 mg; vitamin D/B; intermittent Rivotril/clonazepam, thought to be about 0.5 mg tablets with half-tablet use. **CLIN-001927–001935.**
- **Apr — Medication / response.** The 2017 two-week procyclidine withdrawal is retrospectively recorded as producing rapid return of the outside-left-foot “corn,” greater shiftiness/unrelaxed state and worsening symptoms; missed procyclidine is associated with later left buttock/piriformis tightness. Visual tolerance remains the rate-limiting issue for higher dosing. **CLIN-001914–001925; CLIN-001943–001947.**
- **2018 — genetics remains live.** Neurology records specific PARK2/PRKN as what “we/Prof Healy suspect is the cause,” while considering genetics unlikely to change current management. **CLIN-001950–001957.**
- **Sep — Medication / use.** Procyclidine is stopped fully during a later withdrawal attempt; the later record says it was restarted in early Jan 2019. **CLIN-002099–002135.**
- **Mid-Nov — Prescription / plan.** Neupro patch commenced by the Parkinson nurse, later documented at 10 mg daily. **CLIN-002099–002113.**
- **7 Dec — focal convergence.** “Pain under the left side of my left foot”; objective callus at left fifth MTPJ; targeted fifth-MTPJ offloading. **CLIN-002965–002973.**

## 2019

- **28 Feb — Prescription / current regimen.** Neupro patch 10 mg daily, usually applied within about 20 minutes of getting up; Azilect 1 mg daily; procyclidine 5 mg first thing then 2.5 mg TDS with some variation; CoQ10, folate and vitamins. Rivotril use remains intermittent. **CLIN-002099–002108.**
- **Medication / response and toxicity.** Amantadine had been tried without benefit and with “sad thoughts,” making re-use unlikely. Higher Neupro exposure is associated with impulsivity/compulsion concerns and reported chorea/dyskinesia; at about 12 mg he reported feeling excellent before subsequently feeling unwell, while 10 mg is described as the stable dose. **CLIN-002112–002135.**
- **2019 — treatment strategy.** Neurology describes a tightrope between stiffness/discomfort and impulsivity/compulsions/chorea-dyskinesia, with levodopa to be deferred “as long as is humanly possible.” **CLIN-002147–002166.**
- **GP Podiatry re-referral.** Feet are re-linked to increased tone, abnormal Parkinsonian gait and “clawing feet” with pain. **CLIN-003091–003101.**

## 2020

- **Neurological foot movement.** The foot-throw appearance is classified as **“actually parkinsonian”** because strength is normal; marked sensitivity/excess movement is also recorded. **CLIN-002282–002290.**
- **Medication transition context.** Later longitudinal medication summaries state that Neupro was removed in favour of the COMT/levodopa-related strategy and that procyclidine was fully discontinued well before Oct 2022; the record itself notes errors in some 2021/2022 letters about continued procyclidine use. These later summaries should therefore be read as retrospective medication-history evidence rather than exact contemporaneous stop dates. **See longitudinal medication summary within the 2025 source material.**

## 2021

- **Sep/Oct — Prescription / plan: first levodopa.** Sinemet 62.5 mg BD is introduced because the patient feels undertreated. Kemadrin is then removed because of ocular-pressure/visual concerns and Sinemet is increased to QID. **CLIN-002330–002354.**
- **Medication strategy.** Literal levodopa deferral ends. Neurology nevertheless states that it does not want Sinemet rising significantly and aims to keep dosing **“as absolutely minimal as possible”** in a very young patient. **CLIN-002351–002354.**
- **Medication-history qualification.** Later notes say the procyclidine was already fully gone and that references to it in 2021/2022 letters were erroneous; they also describe intolerance of Kemadrin increases because of reduced eyesight. The exact historical sequence should therefore be taken from the underlying entries rather than inferred from medication lists alone.
- **Measurement point.** The surviving record does not identify a structured foot-by-dose assessment around the major substitution from anticholinergic treatment to Sinemet. This is a documentary absence, not proof that no assessment occurred.

## 2022

- **Morning foot phenotype.** Feet take 20–25 minutes to “warm up,” are sore, with pain around the edges; Sinemet timing varies with functional demands. **CLIN-002378–002386.**
- **Prescription / medication pattern.** The later medication history records Stalevo 50 twice daily by 2022 with Sinemet 62.5 mg PRN no more than once daily, reportedly about 2–3 times weekly by Oct 2022. This is a retrospective summary and should not be treated as an exact dispensing record.

## 2023

- **Mar — Prescription / medication: pregabalin.** GP referral records severe bilateral burning feet/lower legs. Pregabalin began at **25 mg BD** with some benefit and, through subsequent prescribing, had reached **200 mg TDS (600 mg/day)**, described as only just controlling symptoms. **CLIN-002637–002641.**
- **Mar — Medication / contrary report.** Parkinson nurse records that the patient had stopped Parkinson medication for ten days and perceived no Parkinsonian difference. The same note states that Lyrica had been commenced by his GP. **CLIN-002681–002684.**
- **OFF/undertreatment evidence.** Severe foot/leg pain plus shuffling/reduced mobility is worse in mornings or later if Sinemet is not taken; the nurse judges leg pain/shuffling OFF-related and Stalevo is commenced. **CLIN-002574–002582.**
- **Evening medication pattern.** Neurology describes typical OFF protective stance/lack of movement/short steps; Stalevo facilitates darts; insufficient levodopa is considered and evening levodopa/CR options are discussed. **CLIN-002449–002474.**
- **Medication-history summary.** By July, later notes describe one Stalevo 50 in the morning, an optional afternoon Stalevo 50 often omitted unless a high-energy day, and optional Sinemet 62.5 in the evening, reportedly taken about five days a week. Pregabalin/Lyrica is recorded at 600 mg/day during this period.

## 2024

- **Medication regimen.** Later summary records Sinemet 62.5 mg around morning/lunch/evening, with the evening dose moved in Mar 2024 to about 30 minutes before food. Lyrica is documented at **150 mg QID = 600 mg/day**. **CLIN-002708–002709; CLIN-002784.**
- **Medication / response.** Lyrica is reported to remove much of the foot pain, particularly on the right, while toe curling persists during darts. This separates analgesic benefit from persistence of a motor/postural phenomenon. **CLIN-002698–002710.**
- **Medication / adverse-effect constraint.** Even on relatively minimal Sinemet, mild dyskinesia is visible; Neurology identifies excessive dyskinesia as a concern when altering timing/dose. **CLIN-002708–002709.**
- **Aug/Sep onward — Medication / self-directed change.** The patient later reports independently reducing Lyrica after psychological improvement, eventually reaching 50 mg TDS. Neurology states that it had not advised this reduction. **CLIN-002767; CLIN-002779; CLIN-002784.**

## 2025

- **Clinical state.** Severe morning foot pain, mainly left: hobbling from bed, inability to tie runners initially, first ~150 footsteps described as torture. Relief is reported once Parkinson medication reaches the system. **CLIN-002777–002780.**
- **Medication / current use.** Lyrica has fallen from the prior maximum 600 mg/day to **50 mg TDS** through patient-led reduction. **CLIN-002767; CLIN-002779; CLIN-002784.**
- **Medication / response.** Neurology records that once Sinemet “hits his system” the feet feel better from a pain perspective; it also considers that higher Sinemet would probably provide further partial relief but at the cost of more severe dyskinesia. **CLIN-002787–002792.**
- **Prescription / plan.** To avoid simply increasing daytime Parkinson medication, some increase in Lyrica is considered. Because of the overnight medication gap, Sinemet controlled release is prescribed around midnight–1 am: initially half of a “Half Sinemet CR” tablet for about two months, then potentially one whole “Half Sinemet CR” tablet if tolerated. **CLIN-002792–002800.**
- **Mar — Medication / response.** The later complaint-response material records that Sinemet CR had been prescribed/escalated and was providing **“profound relief currently.”** It also acknowledges longstanding Sinemet benefit on the experience of pain while maintaining that this does not identify the primary cause of the pain. **CLIN-003242–003269.**

## 2026

- **4 Feb — genetic confirmation.** St Vincent’s reports two pathogenic Parkin variants and attributes the Parkinsonism and dystonia to compound heterozygous **PRKN** mutations. **CLIN-003220–003228.**

## Medication-sequence summary

The record therefore contains two related but non-identical histories:

**Prescription / treatment strategy:** rasagiline + procyclidine/Kemadrin and intermittent clonazepam → dopamine-agonist/adjunct trials including Neupro and amantadine → first Sinemet in 2021 → constrained levodopa/Stalevo use → Sinemet CR in 2025, alongside pregabalin/Lyrica for the pain pathway.

**Actual use / response:** marked benefit and visual limitation with procyclidine; withdrawal-associated worsening of tension/feet/gait; Neupro benefit complicated by impulsivity/chorea-dyskinesia; amantadine ineffective/intolerable; variable and PRN levodopa use; OFF-related leg/foot phenomena alongside contradictory medication-off evidence; substantial but incomplete pregabalin benefit; worsening after self-reduction of Lyrica; and marked reported improvement after Sinemet CR.

Neither sequence, by itself, determines the mechanism of every foot symptom. Their value is chronological: they show what was prescribed, what was actually used where documented, and what changed with treatment state.
