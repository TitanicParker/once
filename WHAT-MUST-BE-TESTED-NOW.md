# What must be tested now

## Purpose

This document is the testing endpoint for the repository.

The record has now been organised in several different ways: chronology, facts, propositions, decision points, tensions, specialist/referral analysis, independent review, a detailed findings register, and an iterative-reading note explaining why the same unchanged record can produce materially different evidential models on repeated structured reading.

The next step is **not another global narrative**. It is to test the remaining propositions at the dates when they became clinically material.

This page therefore asks the questions that the documents alone cannot finally answer.

It should be read with:

- [Iterative reading and evidential instability](iterative-reading-evidential-instability.md) — why repeated structured reading changes evidential weighting without changing the facts;
- [Record Findings Register](RECORD-FINDINGS-REGISTER.md) — the detailed findings generated by the multi-pass review;
- [Propositions](propositions.md) — propositions raised by the record but not yet established;
- [Decision points](DECISION-POINTS.md) — the dates at which management choices or reassessment became material;
- [Tensions](TENSIONS.md) — the strongest competing evidence that must not be flattened into one account;
- [Specialist input](SPECIALIST-INPUT.md) and [Referral / escalation](REFERRAL-SPECIALIST-ESCALATION.md) — what specialist involvement occurred, what each referral was asked to do, and what may have remained unassigned;
- [Independent neurological review](independent-neurological-review.md) — the current restrained clinical assessment; and
- [Complete record](complete-record.md) — the source record against which every answer must ultimately be checked.

## Why these questions now exist

The iterative-reading problem matters because the evidence is stable while its relationships are not always obvious on a first chronological reading.

A first reading can compress the case into a simple story about delayed levodopa. A structured second reading makes that formulation less satisfactory. It strengthens important defence evidence — particularly the genuine dyskinesia/behavioural treatment limits — while also making some earlier foot-specific medication-state evidence and later re-synthesis questions more important.

The Record Findings Register provides the mechanism for testing that change in understanding. It separates contemporaneous facts from later reconstruction, treatment effect from mechanism, structural pathology from neurological modulation, local updating from longitudinal carry-forward, and documentary silence from proof of omission.

The questions below are therefore designed to prevent a reviewer from answering a different, easier case than the one contained in the record.

# A. Standard-of-care questions

## 1. The 2017 starting position

**Was the initial 2017 strategy — caution with levodopa/dopamine replacement in a very young patient who was already responding to rasagiline/procyclidine and who was considered at risk of dyskinesia — within a reasonable range of neurological practice at the time?**

This must be answered prospectively. The 2025 controlled-release response and the 2026 PRKN result cannot be used as though they were known in 2017.

The question should expressly account for the external opinion from Prof Healy and for the fact that the contemporaneous record itself recognised both possible benefit and possible future regret from delay.

## 2. The 2017–2018 medication-state evidence

**What clinical significance should reasonably have been assigned to the procyclidine withdrawal/rechallenge evidence, including deterioration in gait/lower-limb state and the later recorded rapid return of the outside-left-foot/corn phenomenon?**

The issue is not whether this proved that a corn was caused by Parkinsonism. It did not.

The issue is whether it established a sufficiently important patient-specific fact: that the foot/lower-limb symptom complex was at least partly **neurologically medication-state modifiable**.

## 3. The first re-synthesis threshold

**By 2018–2019, had the combination of medication-state-sensitive lower-limb symptoms, persistent foot problems, procyclidine limitations, failed or limited alternatives, dopamine-agonist benefit, dyskinesia and behavioural toxicity created a reasonable need to re-synthesise the working model or treatment strategy?**

If yes, what should that re-synthesis reasonably have consisted of?

The answer should distinguish:

- more total dopaminergic treatment;
- different timing or distribution;
- a different formulation;
- a symptom-specific treatment trial;
- renewed specialist review; and
- continued observation with no material change.

## 4. The 2021 substitution test

**When procyclidine was removed and Sinemet introduced/increased in 2021, should the adequacy of that substitution reasonably have been tested against the feet and lower-limb symptom domain specifically?**

If so, what would a reasonable structured test have looked like?

The record does not clearly show a formal foot-specific substitution assessment. Documentary silence does not prove that no clinical thought occurred, so the expert must address the significance of the missing visible assessment rather than treating absence as proof.

## 5. The 2022 morning pattern

**Did the reproducible 20–25 minute morning foot “warm-up” pattern in 2022 require structured medication-state assessment, or was it reasonable at that stage to regard it as a non-specific clue in a patient who was otherwise functioning well and who still had known corns/calluses and treatment sensitivity?**

This question must not assume that controlled-release levodopa was already indicated merely because it later helped.

## 6. The later-2023 OFF threshold

**Once pain and shuffling were reported as worse when Sinemet had not been taken and Neurology itself described the presentation as OFF-related/undertreated, what reassessment of dopaminergic architecture should reasonably have followed?**

The expert should address, separately:

- total daily dose;
- individual dose size;
- dose interval;
- consistency/adherence;
- timing relative to food and absorption;
- immediate-release versus longer-duration formulations;
- evening coverage;
- overnight coverage;
- symptom diaries or other structured ON/OFF observation; and
- the dyskinesia/behavioural ceiling on conventional escalation.

This is currently the most important potential standard-of-care testing point in the record.

## 7. Specialist movement-disorders involvement

**At what point, if any, did renewed consultant movement-disorders review become more than merely useful and become a step that a reasonably competent clinician should have arranged?**

The answer must take account of the substantial specialist and multidisciplinary involvement that did occur, including Prof Healy and Parkinson specialist nursing.

It must also take account of referral scope. The surviving documents do not clearly show a later external referral squarely asking one specialist to integrate the entire problem of young-onset parkinsonism, dystonia/toe clawing, structural foot pathology, medication-state responsiveness, pain, OFF phenomena and treatment toxicity.

Any conclusion that consultant movement-disorders co-management was mandatory requires appropriate evidence about the relevant Irish standard of care.

# B. Mechanism questions

## 8. What exactly was medication-state sensitive?

**Which components of the lower-limb/foot syndrome were probably modified by neurological medication state, and which remained independently structural, neuropathic-type, focal mechanical, dystonic/postural or otherwise mediated?**

The expert should resist a single-cause answer unless the evidence genuinely supports one.

The following findings must be reconciled rather than selectively preferred:

- genuine corns/calluses/deformity and structural pathology;
- dystonic-looking gait/posture and toe clawing;
- procyclidine withdrawal/rechallenge effects;
- dyskinesia and behavioural toxicity at higher stimulation;
- severe burning pain in 2023;
- the patient's ten-day medication interruption without an obvious global Parkinson difference;
- later explicit OFF-related pain/shuffling;
- substantial pregabalin pain benefit;
- persistence of toe curling despite pain improvement; and
- later improvement when dopaminergic overnight coverage was extended.

## 9. Pain generation versus pain modulation

**Did dopaminergic treatment primarily alter the generator of pain, the motor/postural conditions producing or loading the pain, central pain experience/modulation, or some combination of these?**

This matters because a strong treatment response is evidence of modifiability but does not automatically identify the original pain generator.

# C. Evidential-continuity questions

## 10. Was there a clinically important carry-forward asymmetry?

**Does the record show that treatment-risk evidence — dyskinesia, behavioural effects and sensitivity to stronger dopaminergic treatment — was carried forward more consistently than earlier evidence that the feet/lower limbs were medication-state responsive?**

If so:

1. was that asymmetry clinically reasonable;
2. did it have any material consequence for later interpretation or treatment; and
3. at what date should earlier foot-specific evidence have been explicitly re-integrated, if at all?

This is a documentary question first and a clinical-significance question second.

A loss of visible evidential continuity does **not** prove forgetting, error or negligence.

## 11. Did later-2023 require global rather than local updating?

**Once OFF-related pain/shuffling had become an explicit working interpretation, should the earlier evidence have been globally re-synthesised rather than handled as separate local observations?**

The relevant earlier evidence includes:

- the original unresolved relationship between structural and neurological foot findings;
- medication-state-sensitive foot/lower-limb change in 2017–2018;
- later treatment toxicity;
- the 2021 substitution;
- the 2022 morning pattern;
- the contradictory March-2023 medication interruption; and
- pregabalin responsiveness.

The test is not whether every earlier clue pointed in one direction. They did not. The test is whether the accumulated conflict itself required a more explicit integrated model.

# D. Observation and documentation questions

## 12. Did clinic observation adequately sample the problem?

**How much weight should be placed on examinations in which the patient appeared relatively well when the record also contains later-day or medication-cycle deterioration that was not necessarily visible at the appointment?**

The expert should address whether a reasonable assessment required more deliberate observation across medication states, symptom diaries, timed review, video, or other practical methods — and whether such methods represented ordinary reasonable practice at the relevant dates.

## 13. What is the significance of the undocumented outcome of the 2024 timing change?

**The 2024 record shows that Sinemet timing relative to food/fluid was adjusted, but the outcome is not clearly documented. What, if anything, can properly be inferred from that gap?**

No inference of failure should be made merely from absence of an outcome note. The issue is whether the absence materially limits the ability to determine whether a potentially important treatment hypothesis was adequately tested.

# E. Causation questions

## 14. When did overnight coverage become a reasonable counterfactual treatment?

**At what earliest date did overnight or longer-duration dopaminergic coverage become a reasonable treatment hypothesis on the information then available?**

The expert should identify the date and the evidence available at that date, rather than reasoning backward from the eventual response.

## 15. What does the 2025 controlled-release response actually prove?

**How strongly does the later reported profound response to Sinemet CR support the proposition that inadequate overnight dopaminergic coverage materially contributed to the then-current severe morning foot-pain state?**

The answer should then separately state what the response does **not** establish, including whether it can prove:

- the same mechanism in earlier years;
- earlier tolerability of the same regimen;
- absence of structural or neuropathic contributors; or
- negligence from earlier non-use.

## 16. Would an earlier reasonable intervention probably have made a material difference?

If an expert identifies a date at which management should reasonably have changed, the next question is:

**On the balance of probabilities, what different management would have occurred, would it probably have been tolerated, and would it probably have reduced pain, motor disability or functional loss to a material degree?**

This must be answered separately from breach.

A finding that further assessment or a different treatment architecture should have been considered does not itself establish that the eventual outcome would probably have been avoided.

## 17. What period of loss, if any, is causally attributable?

**If earlier reassessment would probably have produced benefit, what is the defensible period of avoidable pain/disability?**

The broadest theory — that a different 2017 decision would have prevented the later course — requires substantially more counterfactual support than a narrower theory focused on later-2023 to 2025 treatment architecture.

# F. Retrospective-evidence questions

## 18. How much historical weight should be given to the 2025 complaint response?

**Which propositions in the later complaint response are independently corroborated by contemporaneous records, and which are later retrospective synthesis or reconstruction?**

The expert should not reject retrospective explanation merely because it is retrospective. Nor should later coherence be treated as proof that exactly the same reasoning existed at every earlier decision point.

## 19. What is the proper reach of the 2026 PRKN result?

**What does confirmation of biallelic pathogenic PRKN/Parkin variants legitimately add to the interpretation of the earlier record?**

The result can confirm the later genetic diagnosis and may validate the seriousness of the earlier PARK2 hypothesis. It cannot be back-projected as genetic certainty available to clinicians in 2017–2025, and it cannot by itself classify every historical foot symptom.

# G. Final expert questions

After answering the date-specific questions above, the expert should give separate conclusions on the following matters:

### 20. Breach

**At any identified date, did the care fall below the standard of a reasonably competent neurologist or other relevant clinician?**

For each alleged breach, identify:

- the date or period;
- the evidence available then;
- the reasonable action that should have occurred;
- the principal defence evidence; and
- why the conclusion does not depend on hindsight.

### 21. Causation

**For every breach identified, what consequence probably followed from that breach rather than from the underlying disorder, structural foot disease, neuropathic-type pain, treatment toxicity or ordinary progression?**

### 22. Specialist threshold

**Was any failure to obtain or renew movement-disorders specialist input itself below reasonable practice, or is specialist involvement relevant only as evidence of what a competent reassessment might have involved?**

### 23. The strongest defence

**What is the strongest clinically reasonable account of why the management remained within acceptable practice despite the later benefit from altered coverage?**

The expert should address the genuine narrow therapeutic window, structural pathology, periods of high function, pregabalin response, contradictory 2023 self-withdrawal evidence, existing specialist input and the risk of dyskinesia/behavioural toxicity.

### 24. The strongest criticism

**What is the strongest criticism that survives those defence points without depending on genetic hindsight, outcome reasoning or the assumption that all foot pain had one cause?**

The current documentary material suggests that the most testable criticism is not simply “levodopa should have been started in 2017.” It is whether, particularly once OFF-related pain/shuffling became explicit, the cumulative record required a more systematic re-synthesis of the foot syndrome and a more deliberate use of timing, formulation, duration and specialist movement-disorders input.

That remains a proposition for expert adjudication, not a concluded finding.

# Testing discipline

Every answer should state which of the following it is based on:

1. **Contemporaneous source evidence** — what was actually recorded at the time.
2. **Later factual evidence** — later events that may inform but must not be back-projected as earlier knowledge.
3. **Retrospective explanation** — later accounts of earlier reasoning, with contemporaneous corroboration identified where it exists.
4. **Clinical inference** — an inference drawn from the record rather than a recorded fact.
5. **Expert standard-of-care opinion** — what reasonable practice required at the relevant date.
6. **Counterfactual causation opinion** — what probably would have happened under reasonable alternative management.

The reviewer should also state the strongest contrary evidence for each material conclusion.

This is the practical response to the evidential-instability problem: **do not resolve the case by choosing the most coherent story. Resolve the individual propositions by showing exactly what evidence, at what date, supports each step.**
