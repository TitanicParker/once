01
Expert proposition 01

What exactly remained unresolved in 2017?

The presentation entered Neurology as an integrated but unexplained problem. The GP chronology moved from bilateral foot pain and abnormal gait into initiation difficulty, marked lower-limb tone, toe clawing, worsening dexterity and threatened employment. By late June, the referral did not present “feet” and “neurology” as already separate disorders. CLIN-000018–000039; CLIN-000064–000085; CLIN-000847–000884.

Hospital Neurology documented severe young-onset Parkinsonism, a striking foot-drop-like gait suggestive of dystonic posture and expressly recorded as “NOT FOOT DROP”, alongside pes planus, hammertoes, corns/calluses, burning and foot pain. Neurology did not state that the foot symptoms were unrelated; it concluded the relationship was “Not clear.” CLIN-001085–001149; CLIN-001142–001149.

The patient’s early history also supplied genuine mechanical evidence: foot pain had reduced after leaving bar work and spending less time standing, while leg tension persisted. CLIN-000965–000976. Podiatry later repeatedly documented focal structural pathology, especially recurrent left fifth-MTPJ callus/HD; in December 2018, “pain under the left side of my left foot” converged with objective fifth-MTPJ callus and targeted offloading. CLIN-002965–002973.

What this establishes: both structural and neurological domains were real. The unresolved issue was their relationship: structural deformity and pressure lesions, pain, tone/posture/clawing, gait, loading and possible neuropathic symptoms.

What this does not establish: that all foot pain was neurological, that the fifth-MTPJ lesion was caused by dystonia, that Podiatry was inappropriate, or that levodopa should have started in July 2017.

Expert question: Was “Not clear” a reasonable provisional formulation in July 2017, and what later evidence would ordinarily require that provisional relationship to be expressly reconsidered?

02
Expert proposition 02

What management architecture was adopted in response?

2017–2021: deliberate levodopa deferral

Neurology expressly chose caution with dopamine agonist/levodopa treatment because of exceptionally young onset and concern about rapid dyskinesia and impulse-control effects. The contemporaneous policy was to use alternatives first and likely avoid the usual dopamine treatments “for as long as possible.” CLIN-001166–001180.

At the same time, local/structural foot care was routed to Podiatry. The surviving referral language asks Podiatry to review foot issues; it does not assign Podiatry the neurological causal question. CLIN-000343–000345; CLIN-001225–001229. The GP’s 6 July note that Podiatry would prioritise the case “as Parkinson’s” is important counterevidence against describing this as a total conceptual separation. CLIN-000236–000245.

Healy: compatible with caution, not clear endorsement of indefinite deferral

Galway expressly asked Prof Daniel Healy whether its wish to hold back levodopa should change. Healy replied that the patient “probably has Park II”, recommended PARK2 testing, and said PARK II patients may manage for long periods with relatively low doses of dopamine replacement but become dyskinetic if over-used. CLIN-001720–001760.

Precise proposition: Healy’s advice was compatible with caution but did not clearly endorse an indefinite or open-ended policy of levodopa deferral.

Post-2021: deferral ended; minimisation/constraint continued

Sinemet was introduced in September 2021 because the patient felt undertreated, and Kemadrin was then replaced with Sinemet because of ocular-pressure concerns. CLIN-002330–002338. The literal levodopa-deferral period therefore ended in 2021. After that, the policy became minimisation/constraint: Neurology stated that it did not want Sinemet rising significantly and aimed to keep dosing minimal in very young patients. CLIN-002351–002354.

This prevents the inaccurate formulation that levodopa was “withheld for eight years.”

Expert question: Was the architecture clinically coherent: 2017–2021 deliberate deferral plus parallel structural foot care, followed after 2021 by constrained/minimised levodopa exposure while the inherited mixed pain model remained available?

03
Expert proposition 03

What later evidence materially affected the foot/lower-limb domain?

The later evidence points in both directions. Its force comes from the fact that integrating and separating evidence accumulated simultaneously.

2017 procyclidine: the narrow inference

On treatment, the patient described dramatic improvement in gait and function; during withdrawal, tension increased, the feet became restless and gait deteriorated. CLIN-001770–001813.

The only inference required: by 2017, the lower-limb/foot/gait domain was demonstrably neurological-treatment-state sensitive.

This does not prove that the recurrent fifth-MTPJ lesion was dystonic, that procyclidine altered a corn structurally, that levodopa would reproduce the same effect, or that all foot pain was neurological.

2018–2020: cross-domain evidence

PARK2 remained suspected; the fifth-MTPJ lesion remained objectively recurrent; the GP later re-linked feet to tone, Parkinsonian gait, clawing and pain; and in 2020 Neurology classified the abnormal foot throw as “actually parkinsonian.” These observations make complete independence increasingly difficult without proving a single pain generator.

2021: principal factual hinge — treatment substitution without identified structured foot measurement

Sinemet began and procyclidine/Kemadrin was removed. CLIN-002330–002354. The record does not identify a structured pre/post assessment of the already treatment-sensitive foot/lower-limb phenotype against medication timing, dose onset, wearing-off, overnight absence, morning state/first steps, recurrent focal outside-left/fifth-MTPJ pain, clawing/curling or toe posture, and gait/loading before and after dosing.

That is a documentary absence, not proof that no clinician ever asked about those matters.

Expert issue: whether a major treatment substitution in 2021 was adequately evaluated against an already-known neurological-treatment-state-sensitive lower-limb/foot domain.

2022–2025: increasingly explicit timing and OFF evidence

In 2022 the feet took 20–25 minutes to “warm up” most mornings. In 2023, nursing and Neurology records identified OFF/undertreatment phenomena affecting leg pain, shuffling and evening movement. CLIN-002574–002582; CLIN-002449–002474. By 2025, Neurology recorded feet mainly left, “murder in the feet,” the first 150 steps as “torture,” and relief once Parkinson medication reached the system. CLIN-002767–002800. Bedtime Sinemet CR was then prescribed, and the later response recorded “profound relief currently.” CLIN-003263–003270.

2023 counterevidence: a credibility safeguard

The same record contains severe bilateral “burning” feet/lower legs, extreme burden described as causing a “death wish”, high-dose pregabalin with incomplete control, and the patient’s report of about ten days off Parkinson medication without perceived Parkinsonian difference. CLIN-002637–002641; CLIN-002681–002684.

This is not an inconvenience to the argument; it is one of its safeguards. It prevents reverse-engineering the history around the later Sinemet CR response and requires a genuinely mixed model. In 2024, pain improved with Lyrica while toe curling persisted, further separating pain from posture.

Expert question: Taken together, how strongly did the 2017–2025 sequence establish that important parts of the foot/lower-limb domain were neurological-treatment-state sensitive while other pain components remained structural, neuropathic or otherwise separable?

04
Expert proposition 04

Was the later evidence integrated into a comprehensive re-evaluation, or mainly managed locally within the inherited model?

This is the principal documentary proposition.

The record shows extensive local management change: procyclidine restoration/escalation after withdrawal; dopamine agonists introduced and constrained by chorea/behavioural effects; Sinemet introduced in 2021; Stalevo used for OFF/undertreatment; pregabalin escalated for severe pain; Sinemet timing altered; and Sinemet CR introduced for the overnight/morning pattern. The record therefore cannot support “management never changed.”

Safe documentary finding: the record does not identify a comprehensive re-synthesis of the original 2017 foot–neurology relationship comparable in explicitness to the repeated local treatment changes.

This is not proof that clinicians never reconsidered the issue mentally, orally or in undocumented discussion.

Pregabalin genealogy

The contemporaneous 2023 record says the patient was commenced on Lyrica by his GP for severe foot/leg pain. CLIN-002681–002684. By 2025, Neurology genuinely used Lyrica and Sinemet as alternative treatment levers because more Sinemet was expected to relieve some pain but increase dyskinesia. CLIN-002787–002800. The later complaint response states that Lyrica was introduced “in part to try to spare further Sinemet increase.” CLIN-003265.

Genealogy rule: later Sinemet-sparing use of pregabalin is documented; Sinemet-sparing as part of the original purpose of pregabalin initiation is not established by the contemporaneous initiation record.

The correct classification is retrospective purpose attribution / possible reconstruction, not proven falsity.

2025 complaint response: continuity, not simple contradiction

The response accepts Sinemet-related pain relief, preserves the proposition that Parkinson’s may magnify pain “of whatever cause”, reaches back to the 2017 dopamine-sparing rationale, and uses that rationale to explain the later treatment course. CLIN-003239–003268.

That makes the response probative as evidence of continuity of the management architecture in the clinician’s own retrospective account. The model may be clinically correct; the documentary question is whether it was ever formally returned to an open state and re-tested against the accumulated cross-domain evidence.

Strongest benign interpretation

Structural pathology was real; Podiatry was appropriate; early mechanical evidence existed; procyclidine caused visual toxicity; dopamine agonists caused choreiform/behavioural problems; levodopa later produced dyskinesia at low exposure; pregabalin materially helped pain; OFF phenomena were recognised and treated; and CR was introduced when the overnight pattern became sufficiently explicit. On this view, clinicians reasonably refined separate components of a genuinely mixed syndrome without requiring a single dramatic “global reset.”

Strongest path-dependent interpretation

A provisional 2017 mixed model may have become a durable organising frame capable of absorbing later integrating evidence without requiring the original classification itself to return to an open state: structural lesion → Podiatry; burning pain → neuropathic pathway; neurological drug changes feet → modulation; Parkinsonian foot movement → motor component; OFF pain/shuffling → local levodopa adjustment; Lyrica helps → pain pathway; Sinemet helps → reduced experience of pain “of whatever cause”; CR profoundly helps → successful modulation while primary generator remains separate.

Expert question: Does the longitudinal record show adequate re-evaluation of the original foot–neurology relationship, or principally repeated local management within an inherited mixed model whose underlying classification remained substantially intact?

05
Expert proposition 05

At what point, if any, would reasonable movement-disorders practice have required reopening the original classification?

This is the ultimate expert question and should not be answered documentarily. The relevant threshold evidence includes:

  1. 2017: severe Parkinsonism plus structural foot disease; relationship expressly “Not clear.”
  2. 2017: neurological treatment/withdrawal materially changes feet, gait and tension.
  3. 2017: Healy considers probable PARK2 and describes low-dose dopamine replacement as potentially sustainable but dyskinesia-prone if over-used.
  4. 2017–2019: recurrent left fifth-MTPJ structural pathology remains real.
  5. 2019: GP re-links foot problems to increased tone, abnormal Parkinsonian gait, clawing and pain.
  6. 2020: Neurology classifies abnormal foot throw as Parkinsonian.
  7. 2021: Sinemet replaces the procyclidine-centred strategy, but no structured foot-by-dose assessment is identified.
  8. 2022: morning foot warm-up/soreness appears.
  9. 2023: nurse and Neurology identify OFF/undertreatment phenomena affecting leg pain, shuffling and evening movement.
  10. 2023: strong counterevidence simultaneously persists — bilateral burning, pregabalin benefit, reported ten-day cessation without perceived Parkinsonian difference.
  11. 2024: pain improves while toe curling persists.
  12. 2025: morning/overnight pattern is explicitly mapped; feet improve after Parkinson medication; Sinemet CR produces reported profound relief.
  13. 2025: complaint response preserves the inherited separate-cause/magnification model while retrospectively defending the original dopamine-sparing rationale.
  14. 2026: two pathogenic PRKN variants confirm Parkin-related Parkinsonism/dystonia. CLIN-003220–003228.

The 2026 genetics result cannot be back-projected into 2017 knowledge. It validates the early PARK2 branch but does not establish the mechanism of the corn, burning pain or every later foot symptom.

Ultimate expert question: At what point, if any, did the accumulated evidence make continued management within the inherited mixed structural/peripheral/neurological model insufficient without an explicit integrated re-evaluation?

If such a threshold existed, an expert should specify what a reasonable reassessment would ordinarily have included: structured ON/OFF examination; symptom-by-dose diary; onset/wearing-off mapping; overnight/morning assessment; separate measures of focal pain, burning pain, toe posture and gait; neurological–podiatric biomechanical review; reassessment of the peripheral-neuropathy hypothesis; and renewed movement-disorders/genetic review.

The expert should also address whether the substantial treatment risks — ocular toxicity, behavioural/impulse effects, chorea and levodopa dyskinesia — reasonably justified continued caution even if a reassessment threshold had been crossed.

Strongest surviving formulation

What survives adversarial testing

An initially reasonable unresolved mixed structural–neurological formulation led to parallel foot and neurological management and deliberate levodopa deferral. Literal levodopa deferral ended in 2021 and was replaced by constrained/minimised dopaminergic treatment. Across the same period, increasingly specific evidence showed that clinically important parts of the foot/lower-limb domain were neurological-treatment-state sensitive, while other pain components remained structurally or analgesically separable. The record documents repeated local treatment adaptation but does not identify a comparably explicit comprehensive re-synthesis of the original foot–neurology relationship. The specialist question is whether, and at what point, reasonable movement-disorders practice required that relationship to be explicitly re-evaluated rather than continuing to manage new evidence within the inherited mixed model.

This formulation does not depend on proving bad faith, a unitary pain mechanism, an obviously wrong 2017 decision, inappropriate Podiatry care, eight years of levodopa withholding, or an inevitable earlier Sinemet CR solution.
