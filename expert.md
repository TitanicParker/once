FINAL MOVEMENT-DISORDERS EXPERT QUESTIONS

1. INITIAL REFERRAL — WAS THE FOOT/LOWER-LIMB SYNDROME ALREADY PART OF THE PRESENTING NEUROLOGICAL PHENOTYPE?

Given that the pre-diagnostic GP referral of 29 June 2017 already documented an unusual stiff/festinant gait, difficulty initiating movement, increased tone in all four limbs, brisk reflexes, inability to relax, toe clawing, bilateral foot problems and progressive functional impairment, how should a movement-disorders neurologist have understood the clinical phenotype being referred?

In particular, should the subsequent dystonic-looking lower-limb examination and suspected PRKN/PARK2 phenotype have led Neurology to treat the longstanding foot/lower-limb syndrome as potentially integral to the movement disorder until demonstrated otherwise, rather than allowing its relationship to remain clinically compartmentalised as “Not clear”?

The expert should distinguish between the existence of genuine structural foot pathology and the separate question of whether abnormal tone, dystonia, gait or loading contributed to the way that pathology was produced, experienced or perpetuated.


2. NEUROLOGY’S OWN FINDINGS — WHAT SHOULD THE EARLIEST SPECIALIST EXAMINATION HAVE ADDED TO THE FORMULATION?

Having received that referral, what significance should be attached to the treating neurologist’s own earliest findings of:

• a “strikingly abnormal” gait;
• a foot-drop-like appearance specifically described as “suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP”;
• absent arm swing;
• bilateral lead-pipe rigidity;
• axial rigidity;
• gross bradykinesia;
• brisk reflexes;
• minimal tremor; and
• very young age at presentation?

Taken together with the GP’s preceding observations of toe clawing, inability to relax, abnormal gait and bilateral foot problems, did these findings provide converging evidence that the lower-limb/foot syndrome should remain actively within the neurological formulation?

If so, was it clinically adequate simply to record the relationship between the Parkinsonian disorder and the corns, calluses, burning and foot symptoms as “Not clear”, or should there have been an explicit plan to investigate whether dystonia, abnormal tone, altered gait or abnormal loading contributed to the recurrent foot problem?


3. MOVEMENT-DISORDERS EXPERTISE — WHEN DID THE CASE REQUIRE SPECIALIST ASSESSMENT OR CO-MANAGEMENT?

At what point did the combination of:

• unusually young onset;
• relatively symmetrical akinetic-rigid parkinsonism;
• minimal tremor;
• brisk reflexes;
• lower-limb dystonic posturing;
• longstanding foot manifestations;
• preserved cognition;
• absence of a dominant family history;
• diagnostic uncertainty;
• suspected recessive/monogenic Parkinsonism; and
• unusually consequential decisions about dopamine replacement

reasonably require formal movement-disorders specialist assessment or continuing specialist co-management?

What significance should be attached to the treating neurologist’s own recognition that the phenotype raised PRKN/PARK2 while expressly stating:

“I am not an expert on these conditions (I am not a movement disorders neurologist)”?

Was that acknowledgement particularly important because the unresolved diagnostic questions were directly linked to management — including interpretation of lower-limb dystonia, possible levodopa sensitivity and the intention to defer dopamine replacement for as long as possible?

And once Prof Daniel Healy independently concluded that the patient “probably has Park II”, recommended PARK2 testing, documented dramatic treatment responsiveness and remained willing to review again, should movement-disorders input have become an ongoing component of management rather than principally a one-off genetic/treatment opinion?


4. PROCYCLIDINE — WHAT NEW CLINICAL INFORMATION DID THE 2017–2018 WITHDRAWAL/RECHALLENGE GENERATE?

What clinical significance should have been attributed to the dramatic improvement with procyclidine and the subsequent planned withdrawal, during which the patient contemporaneously reported increasing tension, restless feet and deterioration of gait?

What additional significance arises from the treating neurologist’s later documentation that during the withdrawal the outside-left-foot/corn phenomenon was the first thing noticed changing rapidly?

Did this within-patient treatment-state experiment establish a new patient-specific proposition: that at least part of the foot symptom complex was neurologically medication-state modifiable?

If so:

• how much evidential weight should that finding have carried;
• should it have altered the earlier “Not clear” formulation;
• and should it have remained an active premise whenever the same or closely related foot problem later recurred?

The expert should not assume from treatment response alone that the corn itself was neurologically caused, that every pain episode had one mechanism, or that procyclidine and levodopa necessarily acted through the same mechanism.


5. LEVODOPA DEFERRAL — DID THE NEW EVIDENCE REQUIRE THE ORIGINAL MANAGEMENT PLAN TO BE RE-TESTED?

The original strategy was to be extremely cautious with dopamine replacement and to leave levodopa or dopamine agonists off for as long as possible.

At the same time, the treating neurologist expressly acknowledged that levodopa might work very well and sustainably and that “in time to come — we may regret that we did not start this earlier.”

Once the record contained:

• a dystonic lower-limb phenotype;
• a serious PRKN/PARK2 hypothesis;
• Prof Healy’s PARK2 assessment;
• marked symptomatic responsiveness;
• and direct evidence that the feet themselves changed with neurological medication state,

should the original levodopa-deferral policy have been explicitly re-evaluated?

In particular, would a carefully observed dopaminergic trial have been clinically reasonable not simply as treatment, but as a diagnostic/therapeutic probe asking:

DOES THE LONGSTANDING FOOT SYNDROME ITSELF CHANGE WITH DOPAMINERGIC STATE?

If such a trial was clinically reasonable, what potentially management-relevant information might have been obtained or forgone by not performing it at that stage?

The question is not whether levodopa necessarily should have been commenced in 2017, but whether the informational cost of continued non-testing formed part of the risk-benefit assessment.


6. THE 2021 THERAPEUTIC SUBSTITUTION — WAS SINEMET EVER SHOWN TO REPLACE THE FOOT-SPECIFIC EFFECT OF PROCYCLIDINE?

When procyclidine was withdrawn because of ocular concerns and immediate-release Sinemet was introduced as its replacement, was the therapeutic substitution ever demonstrated to reproduce the foot-specific benefit or control associated with the previous medication state?

The expectation that Sinemet would “more than compensate” for Kemadrin was a prediction.

Was that prediction actually tested against the feet as a distinct symptom domain?

Or was adequacy of replacement inferred mainly from general Parkinsonian motor function and tolerability?

The essential sequence is:

KNOWN FOOT MEDICATION-STATE RESPONSE
→ PROCYCLIDINE REMOVED
→ SINEMET INTRODUCED AS REPLACEMENT
→ COMPENSATION EXPECTED
→ WAS COMPENSATION OF THE FEET EVER DEMONSTRATED?

If not, what significance should attach to subsequent recurrence of morning and later-day foot symptoms?


7. PREGABALIN — WHAT DID ITS RESPONSE ESTABLISH, AND WHAT MANAGEMENT ROLE DID IT LATER ACQUIRE?

Given that pregabalin was introduced in the context of severe burning/neuropathic-type foot and lower-leg pain, what does its subsequent response legitimately establish?

How should the expert interpret the sequence of:

• initial benefit at low dose;
• later severe pain despite very high-dose treatment;
• subsequent substantial analgesic benefit;
• incomplete control despite high-dose treatment;
• improvement in pain while toe curling/clawing persisted;
• major self-reduction followed by worsening pain; and
• simultaneous later evidence that levodopa state also materially altered pain?

Does responsiveness to pregabalin establish a peripheral neuropathic generator, or only that part of the pain burden was pharmacologically modifiable?

How should substantial pregabalin benefit be reconciled with persistent motor/postural phenomena, later OFF-related pain, Sinemet-associated pain relief and the subsequent profound response to overnight Sinemet CR?

Finally, what weight should be given to the later retrospective statement that pregabalin was used partly to spare further Sinemet increase, given that the contemporaneous initiation record describes treatment of severe burning/neuropathic-type pain rather than a documented original levodopa-sparing strategy?

The expert should distinguish:

WHY PREGABALIN WAS STARTED
→ WHAT IT ACTUALLY ACHIEVED
→ WHAT ITS RESPONSE SAYS ABOUT MECHANISM
→ WHAT MANAGEMENT FUNCTION IT LATER ACQUIRED.


8. 2022–2024 — DID THE EMERGING PATTERN INDICATE A PROBLEM OF DOPAMINERGIC DISTRIBUTION RATHER THAN SIMPLY DOSE?

Following the 2021 substitution, what significance should have been attached to the combination of:

• a reproducible 20–25-minute morning foot “warm-up” in 2022;
• flexible or demand-related immediate-release Sinemet use;
• morning and evening deterioration;
• later pain and shuffling associated with lower medication states;
• dyskinesia at higher dopaminergic states;
• substantial pregabalin pain relief with persistent toe curling; and
• continuing functional limitation?

Could this pattern indicate that the therapeutic problem was not simply “more versus less levodopa”, but the temporal distribution of dopaminergic stimulation?

Specifically, could the patient have occupied a narrow therapeutic window in which:

• troughs permitted pain, dystonia, rigidity, shuffling or abnormal loading;
• an intermediate dopaminergic state improved movement and pain;
• and higher states produced dyskinesia?

At what point should the following have been considered independently:

• total daily dose;
• individual dose size;
• dosing interval;
• consistency of administration;
• relationship to food and absorption;
• immediate-release versus longer-duration formulation; and
• duration of overnight coverage?


9. LATER 2023 — WHEN DID THE CUMULATIVE EVIDENCE REQUIRE GLOBAL RE-SYNTHESIS?

Once the record expressly documented worsening pain and shuffling when Sinemet had not been taken, and Neurology itself concluded that the patient appeared undertreated and that the leg pain and shuffling were OFF-related, what should have happened to the overall explanatory and treatment model?

Did that evidence require explicit re-synthesis of:

• the original “Not clear” foot-causation proposition;
• the initial GP description of toe clawing and abnormal gait;
• the neurologist’s documented dystonic lower-limb posture;
• the suspected PRKN/PARK2 phenotype;
• the 2017 procyclidine withdrawal;
• the 2018 focal outside-left-foot return;
• the 2021 procyclidine-to-Sinemet substitution;
• the 2022 morning pattern;
• persistent structural Podiatry pathology;
• pregabalin responsiveness; and
• established dyskinesia sensitivity?

Was later 2023 the point at which medication-state sensitivity should have become a stable longitudinal premise rather than a series of locally observed findings?

If so, should the full 24-hour dopaminergic regimen — including daytime troughs, consistency of dosing, formulation and the prolonged overnight gap — have been reviewed?

The expert should also address the contrary March 2023 ten-day self-withdrawal report and explain what evidential weight that uncontrolled observation should receive relative to the later more symptom-specific ON/OFF observations.


10. THE 2025 SINEMET CR RESPONSE — WHAT DOES “PROFOUND RELIEF” ESTABLISH, AND HOW FAR BACK MAY IT LEGITIMATELY REACH?

Given that the patient had already received daytime immediate-release levodopa for several years, what is the clinical significance of the reported “profound relief” after controlled-release Sinemet was introduced to extend dopaminergic coverage through the previously untreated overnight interval?

Does that response provide strong patient-specific evidence that inadequate temporal dopaminergic coverage — particularly the prolonged overnight trough — was materially contributing to the then-current morning foot-pain burden?

Does it support a therapeutic-window model in which:

• low dopaminergic states permitted pain, dystonia, shuffling, rigidity or abnormal loading;
• an intermediate therapeutic state improved pain and movement;
• and higher dopaminergic states produced dyskinesia?

What does the magnitude of that response reveal about the adequacy of the preceding 24-hour treatment architecture?

How far may this later natural experiment legitimately strengthen the significance of:

• the 2022 morning “warm-up” phenomenon;
• the later-2023 OFF-related pain evidence;
• the 2021 substitution question;
• and the earlier procyclidine medication-state findings?

And what must remain unproven, including:

• the exact biological pain generator;
• the proposition that every historical episode of foot pain shared one mechanism;
• the proposition that controlled-release formulation alone, rather than increased duration of dopaminergic exposure, caused the benefit;
• and the counterfactual claim that Sinemet CR would necessarily have produced the same result if introduced years earlier?


OVERARCHING EXPERT QUESTION

From 2017 to 2025, was the policy of prolonged dopaminergic restraint repeatedly re-evaluated against accumulating patient-specific evidence of both treatment benefit and treatment risk, or did the original policy retain governing force after the evidential circumstances had materially changed?

More specifically:

Was the management plan treated as a hypothesis that accumulating patient-specific evidence was permitted to revise, or as a governing framework within which new findings were accommodated without materially reopening the underlying diagnostic and therapeutic assumptions?

And was there an evidential asymmetry whereby patient-specific evidence of treatment risk — dyskinesia, behavioural effects and levodopa sensitivity — remained strongly active in later decision-making, while earlier patient-specific evidence of foot/lower-limb treatment responsiveness had local treatment force without equivalent durable influence on the longitudinal model?
