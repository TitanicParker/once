# Independent Neurological Review: Longitudinal Management and Professional-Standard Issues

## Scope and purpose

This document provides a restrained, independent documentary assessment of the longitudinal neurological management of a very-young-onset Parkinsonian syndrome with lower-limb/foot symptoms, treatment responsiveness, treatment toxicity, later OFF-state features, and subsequent confirmation of biallelic **PRKN/Parkin disease**.

The assessment is framed by the professional and clinical standards reasonably applicable in Ireland during the relevant period, approximately 2017–2025. It is not intended to advocate for either the patient or the treating clinicians.

The central question is not whether levodopa should simply have been prescribed earlier. It is:

> At what point, if any, should accumulating patient-specific evidence have caused a reasonably competent neurologist to reconsider the working model of the foot/lower-limb syndrome, the timing or distribution of dopaminergic treatment, or the need for movement-disorders specialist input?

The analysis is prospective. Later genetic confirmation is not back-projected as knowledge clinicians possessed at earlier dates.

---

## Applicable professional-standard framework

### Medical Council of Ireland

The relevant Medical Council guidance during most of the period was the **Guide to Professional Conduct and Ethics for Registered Medical Practitioners, 8th edition (2016, amended 2019)**, replaced from 1 January 2024 by the 9th edition.

A materially relevant principle is the requirement that doctors maintain competence, recognise the limits of their expertise, and seek appropriate assistance where necessary to provide safe care.

This does not mean that every patient with Parkinsonism must be managed by a dedicated movement-disorders neurologist. It does mean that the significance of referral or co-management increases where:

- the presentation is unusually young or atypical;
- genetic Parkinsonism is suspected;
- the treating neurologist has acknowledged limits in movement-disorders expertise;
- management involves difficult treatment-response and treatment-toxicity trade-offs.

### NICE NG71, Parkinson's disease in adults

NICE guidance is not binding in Ireland but is relevant evidence of accepted contemporary neurological practice where Irish-specific guidance is limited.

The most relevant recommendations are:

- treatment should be individualised according to symptoms, clinical circumstances, patient goals, benefits and harms;
- levodopa should be offered where motor symptoms materially affect quality of life;
- the diagnosis should be reviewed if atypical features develop;
- where dyskinesia or motor fluctuations/wearing-off develop, advice should be sought from a clinician with specialist Parkinson's expertise before modifying therapy;
- nocturnal akinesia may warrant consideration of dopaminergic treatment directed at night-time coverage.

These recommendations do not establish that controlled-release levodopa was required years before it was used. They do establish that medication timing, motor fluctuations and specialist Parkinson's input were recognised management issues during the relevant period.

### PRKN/Parkin disease literature

Contemporary and later movement-disorders literature recognises that biallelic PRKN disease may present with:

- very young onset;
- dystonia, often affecting the lower limbs;
- marked dopaminergic responsiveness;
- relatively preserved cognition;
- substantial risk of dyskinesia at comparatively low dopaminergic exposure.

The later 2026 genetic result therefore confirms that the 2017 PARK2/PRKN hypothesis was clinically substantive. It does not establish that the diagnosis should have been certain in 2017.

# Decision-point analysis

## 1. July 2017: initial neurological formulation

### Evidence available at the time

The record documented:

- bilateral foot problems;
- abnormal gait;
- toe clawing;
- difficulty initiating movement;
- increased tone;
- brisk reflexes;
- progressive functional deterioration;
- a strikingly abnormal gait;
- a dystonic-looking foot-drop-like posture rather than true foot drop;
- bilateral rigidity;
- axial rigidity;
- marked bradykinesia;
- minimal tremor;
- genuine structural foot abnormalities.

Neurology diagnosed young-onset Parkinsonism while retaining a broad differential and recorded the relationship between the neurological syndrome and the foot symptoms as uncertain.

### Reasonable clinical interpretations

Reasonable possibilities included:

1. predominantly structural/podiatric foot pain;
2. neurological dystonia or abnormal tone contributing to posture, gait or loading;
3. a mixed structural, neurological and pain syndrome.

The available evidence did not require the conclusion that all foot pain was neurological.

### Reasonable management options

Reasonable options included:

- neurological investigation;
- podiatry involvement;
- conservative symptomatic treatment;
- continued observation of the relationship between the movement disorder and the feet.

### Professional-standard assessment

**Clearly/probably within reasonable practice.**

The initial uncertainty was clinically defensible. The important point was that it remained a revisable proposition rather than a permanent conclusion.

### Reassessment trigger

Not yet.

### Specialist threshold

Movement-disorders input was available and potentially useful, but not yet clearly required.

---

## 2. July–September 2017: suspected PARK2 and dopaminergic restraint

The treating neurologist recognised features suggestive of recessive/PARK2 Parkinsonism and recorded that he was not a movement-disorders neurologist.

A second opinion was appropriately sought from Prof Daniel Healy. He considered PARK2 likely, recommended genetic testing, documented marked treatment responsiveness, described the possibility of sustained benefit on relatively low dopamine-replacement doses, and also warned of dyskinesia if treatment was excessive.

The treating neurologist adopted a deliberately cautious strategy toward levodopa and dopamine agonists because of very young age and anticipated treatment complications.

### Professional-standard assessment

**Probably within reasonable practice / legitimate clinical-judgment territory.**

A criticism based simply on failure to start levodopa in 2017 is weak.

The more important professional requirement was that the treatment strategy remain open to revision if the evidential position changed.

### Specialist threshold

Specialist input was obtained appropriately. Continuing co-management was not yet clearly mandatory.

---

## 3. Late 2017–April 2018: procyclidine withdrawal and rechallenge

Procyclidine produced marked functional improvement.

During planned withdrawal, the patient reported:

- increasing bodily tension;
- restless feet;
- worsening gait.

Treatment was reintroduced, and lower-dose reintroduction did not control all symptoms.

In April 2018 the treating neurologist recorded a more specific observation: during withdrawal, the outside-left-foot/corn phenomenon was the first thing noticed to change rapidly.

### Clinical significance

This is meaningful within-patient treatment-state evidence.

It supports the proposition that at least part of the lower-limb/foot symptom complex was modifiable by neurological medication state.

It does not prove that:

- the corn itself was caused by Parkinsonism;
- all foot pain was neurological;
- procyclidine and levodopa would have produced the same effect.

### Professional-standard assessment

Continued procyclidine and continued restraint with levodopa remained defensible.

However, the earlier formulation that the neurological relationship was simply “not clear” should by this stage reasonably have evolved into a more explicit working proposition:

> at least some lower-limb/foot symptoms appear medication-state responsive, although the mechanism remains uncertain.

### Reassessment trigger

**Yes.**

This was the first material longitudinal trigger to revise the explanatory model.

### Specialist threshold

Renewed movement-disorders review was **clinically advisable**, though not clearly mandatory.

---

## 4. 2018–2019: treatment toxicity becomes patient-specific

Higher-dose dopaminergic therapy produced:

- dyskinesia/chorea;
- behavioural or compulsive effects;
- a difficult balance between inadequate benefit and excessive stimulation.

The treating neurologist described this as an extremely difficult therapeutic balance.

### Professional-standard assessment

**Clearly defensible.**

The emergence of real treatment toxicity materially strengthens the justification for caution.

This was not a case in which escalation was obviously risk-free.

### Reassessment trigger

Yes, but in a different direction: the problem increasingly resembled a **narrow therapeutic window**, with clinically relevant problems both at lower and higher dopaminergic states.

### Specialist threshold

By this stage renewed movement-disorders involvement was **strongly indicated**.

The combination of:

- very-young-onset disease;
- suspected monogenic Parkinsonism;
- dystonia;
- strong treatment response;
- dyskinesia/chorea;
- behavioural toxicity;
- acknowledged limits in movement-disorders expertise

created a reasonable basis for specialist co-management.

Whether such co-management was professionally mandatory is less certain.

---

## 5. 2021: levodopa introduction and substitution for procyclidine

Levodopa was introduced when the patient was considered undertreated.

Procyclidine was subsequently withdrawn because of ocular-pressure concerns, and Sinemet was increased as its replacement.

The expectation was that the increased Sinemet would compensate for removal of procyclidine.

### Professional-standard assessment

**Probably within reasonable practice.**

There is no identified standard requiring a formal foot-specific scoring protocol after such a substitution.

However, because the feet had previously demonstrated medication-state responsiveness, recognised good practice would favour checking explicitly whether the lower-limb/foot benefit previously associated with procyclidine remained controlled after substitution.

The surviving record does not clearly demonstrate such a structured symptom-specific reassessment.

### Reassessment trigger

Potentially yes, depending on subsequent symptom recurrence.

---

## 6. 2022: reproducible morning foot “warm-up”

The record documented a recurring morning period in which the feet took approximately 20–25 minutes to “warm up”.

Sinemet use was also described as flexible and partly adjusted to anticipated activity.

At the same time, dyskinesia sensitivity remained evident even at relatively low dopaminergic exposure.

### Reasonable interpretations

The morning phenomenon could reasonably have reflected:

- structural/mechanical stiffness;
- early-morning OFF dystonia or rigidity;
- delayed medication effect;
- more than one of these mechanisms.

The phrase “warm up” alone did not prove OFF dystonia.

### Professional-standard assessment

**Borderline / dependent upon clinical judgment.**

Given the prior history, the morning pattern reasonably warranted specific questioning about:

- timing of the previous evening dose;
- timing of the morning dose;
- time to relief;
- relation to missed or delayed medication;
- evening deterioration;
- whether symptoms were motor, dystonic, painful or mechanical.

No specific guideline required controlled-release levodopa at this stage.

### Specialist threshold

Movement-disorders input remained **strongly indicated**, particularly because trough symptoms and peak-dose toxicity were both relevant.

---

## 7. March 2023: contrary ten-day medication-withdrawal report

The patient reported approximately ten days off Parkinson medication without perceiving a meaningful difference “from a Parkinson's perspective”.

This evidence points against a simple model in which all lower-limb symptoms were consistently dopaminergic.

### Professional-standard assessment

A clinician who gave this observation substantial weight would have been acting within reasonable practice.

However, the observation was:

- uncontrolled;
- self-directed;
- global rather than symptom-specific;
- potentially confounded by other variables.

It should therefore have reduced confidence in a simple medication-state explanation, but not necessarily closed the issue.

### Reassessment trigger

The conflicting evidence created a need for more symptom-specific testing rather than a definitive conclusion in either direction.

---

## 8. Later 2023: pain and shuffling classified as OFF-related

Later in 2023 the record documented:

- severe foot pain;
- reduced mobility;
- shuffling;
- worsening in the morning and later in the day when Sinemet had not been taken.

Neurology itself concluded that the patient appeared undertreated and that the leg pain and shuffling were OFF-related.

Other records described evening short-stepped or protective movement states as typical OFF symptoms, and levodopa-containing medication was being used both for mobility and pain.

### Professional-standard significance

This is the clearest point at which the evidential position materially changed.

At this stage a reasonable longitudinal model needed to accommodate:

- genuine structural foot pathology;
- persistent dystonic/postural abnormalities;
- pregabalin-responsive pain;
- prior medication-state lower-limb response;
- current OFF-related pain and shuffling;
- dyskinesia at higher dopaminergic states.

A mixed structural, neurological, pain and medication-state model was more reasonable than a single-mechanism account.

### Narrow therapeutic-window hypothesis

By this point the following model was clinically plausible:

**low dopaminergic state**
- pain;
- stiffness;
- dystonia;
- shuffling;
- abnormal loading;

**intermediate state**
- improved movement and possibly reduced pain;

**higher dopaminergic state**
- dyskinesia/chorea;
- behavioural toxicity.

This did not prove the mechanism of every historical foot-pain episode.

### Professional-standard assessment

**Arguably below reasonable practice** if the OFF interpretation was not followed by explicit reassessment of:

- total daily dose;
- individual dose size;
- dose intervals;
- adherence and timing;
- food/absorption;
- evening dosing;
- duration of overnight coverage;
- formulation.

The criticism is stronger if these issues were managed without specialist Parkinson's/movement-disorders input.

### Reassessment trigger

**Strongly yes.**

This is the clearest point at which medication-state sensitivity should have become a stable longitudinal premise.

---

## 9. Specialist involvement once dyskinesia and fluctuations were established

NICE guidance in force from July 2017 advised seeking specialist Parkinson's expertise before modifying treatment once dyskinesia or motor fluctuations/wearing-off had developed.

The record demonstrates both phenomena.

The existence of Parkinson's specialist nursing involvement is an important defence and may have provided relevant expertise.

However, the patient-specific context remained unusual:

- very-young-onset disease;
- suspected genetic Parkinsonism;
- dystonia;
- narrow treatment window;
- dyskinesia/chorea;
- behavioural effects;
- pain and OFF phenomena;
- medication timing and formulation questions.

### Professional-standard assessment

By later 2023, movement-disorders specialist input was **strongly indicated**.

Whether consultant movement-disorders co-management was strictly required to remain within reasonable Irish professional practice cannot be determined with confidence from the documents alone. That issue requires expert evidence concerning Irish neurology practice and the expertise available within the treating service.

---

## 10. Pregabalin responsiveness and persistent toe curling

Pregabalin materially reduced pain, while toe curling/clawing and functional limitations persisted.

### Interpretation

Pregabalin responsiveness demonstrates pain modulation.

It does not prove a purely peripheral neuropathic generator.

Persistent toe curling despite analgesic improvement supports separating:

- pain mechanism;
- dystonia/posture;
- structural pathology;
- dopaminergic state.

### Professional-standard assessment

Use of pregabalin was clinically defensible where it produced meaningful benefit.

The relevant criticism would arise only if its success displaced further assessment of persistent motor/postural or OFF-state phenomena.

---

## 11. 2025: overnight dopaminergic gap and controlled-release Sinemet

By 2025 the record documented:

- severe morning foot pain;
- marked difficulty with the first steps after waking;
- relief when Parkinson medication took effect;
- a prolonged overnight interval after the last evening Sinemet dose.

Controlled-release Sinemet was then introduced at bedtime.

Subsequent documentation described “profound relief”.

### Clinical significance

This strongly supports the proposition that insufficient overnight dopaminergic coverage materially contributed to the then-current morning syndrome.

The key treatment change was not necessarily controlled-release formulation in isolation. It was the extension and redistribution of dopaminergic coverage across the overnight period.

### Historical reach

The 2025 response legitimately strengthens the significance of:

- the 2022 morning warm-up;
- the later-2023 OFF-related symptoms;
- the broader hypothesis that timing and duration of dopaminergic coverage mattered.

It does not establish:

- that every historical foot-pain episode had one mechanism;
- that controlled-release levodopa would necessarily have worked years earlier;
- that structural pathology was irrelevant;
- that the same pain generator operated continuously from 2017 onward.

### Professional-standard assessment

The 2025 intervention itself appears reasonable.

The principal longitudinal question is whether a temporal-coverage problem should have been investigated more explicitly by later 2023.

---

# Longitudinal evidential asymmetry

The record supports the proposition that treatment-risk evidence retained strong longitudinal influence.

Repeatedly documented risks included:

- ocular/visual effects from procyclidine;
- dopamine-agonist behavioural effects;
- dyskinesia/chorea;
- low-dose levodopa sensitivity;
- concern about dopamine dysregulation.

The record also contains repeated treatment-benefit evidence affecting the lower limbs:

- marked procyclidine response;
- deterioration during withdrawal;
- rapid focal foot change on withdrawal;
- morning lower-limb difficulty;
- later OFF-related pain and shuffling;
- benefit from dopaminergic medication;
- strong response to extended overnight coverage.

The benefit evidence appears less clearly incorporated into the longitudinal treatment model until later years.

Initially that asymmetry was clinically understandable because:

- treatment toxicity directly constrained prescribing;
- the mechanism of the foot pain was genuinely uncertain;
- structural disease was real.

By later 2023 the asymmetry becomes more difficult to justify because the neurological team itself had classified pain and shuffling as OFF-related.

# Specialist-referral progression

## Optional

**July 2017**

Movement-disorders input was useful but not yet clearly required.

## Clinically advisable

**September 2017–April 2018**

The threshold rose after:

- specific PARK2 suspicion;
- acknowledged limits in movement-disorders expertise;
- Prof Healy's opinion;
- medication-state lower-limb evidence.

## Strongly indicated

**By 2019**

The combination of:

- very-young-onset disease;
- suspected monogenic Parkinsonism;
- strong treatment response;
- dyskinesia/chorea;
- behavioural toxicity;
- difficult treatment balance

made renewed specialist involvement strongly indicated.

The case for specialist input became still stronger from 2021–2023 as levodopa, dyskinesia, fluctuating symptoms, pain and dosing architecture interacted.

## Professionally required

Specialist Parkinson's expertise was supported by contemporaneous NICE guidance once dyskinesia and motor fluctuations/wearing-off were being treated.

Whether this translated into a mandatory requirement for consultant movement-disorders co-management in the relevant Irish service is not established by the documentary material alone.

# Causation

Professional-standard questions should remain separate from causation.

## Biological causation

The record supports the conclusion that some lower-limb symptoms were neurological and medication-state responsive.

It also supports genuine structural foot pathology and a pain component that responded materially to pregabalin.

A mixed mechanism is therefore more defensible than a single causal explanation.

## Treatment causation

The 2025 controlled-release Sinemet response strongly supports a causal contribution from overnight under-coverage to the then-current morning syndrome.

It does not establish that earlier levodopa, or earlier controlled-release levodopa, would probably have prevented the overall pain syndrome.

## Avoidable duration or severity

It is plausible that earlier identification of medication-state trough symptoms could have reduced some period of pain, stiffness, shuffling or morning disability.

The available record does not establish that this would probably have occurred over a defined earlier period.

The counterfactual remains uncertain because of:

- structural foot disease;
- pregabalin responsiveness;
- treatment-limiting dyskinesia;
- behavioural toxicity;
- uncertainty regarding the regimen a specialist would have selected;
- uncertainty about tolerability.

# Final opinion

## 1. Strongly supported findings

- The initial 2017 presentation was unusually young and atypical.
- PARK2/PRKN disease was a genuine contemporaneous diagnostic hypothesis.
- Appropriate external specialist input was obtained in 2017.
- Initial caution with dopaminergic therapy was clinically defensible.
- Treatment toxicity later became real and clinically important.
- The lower limbs/feet generated repeated treatment-state observations.
- By later 2023 Neurology itself classified pain and shuffling as OFF-related.
- By 2025 a prolonged overnight medication gap was identified and extending coverage produced marked benefit.
- The 2026 PRKN confirmation cannot be back-projected as earlier certainty.

## 2. Reasonable-clinical-judgment territory

Competent neurologists could reasonably disagree about:

- whether levodopa should have been started in 2017;
- how much weight to give the procyclidine withdrawal/rechallenge;
- the relative contribution of structural, dystonic, neuropathic and dopaminergic mechanisms;
- whether the 2021 substitution adequately reproduced foot-specific benefit;
- whether the 2022 morning warm-up should have triggered overnight treatment;
- how much weight to give the ten-day medication withdrawal in 2023;
- which dopaminergic formulation would have been optimal.

## 3. Potential departures from reasonable practice

### 2018
A relatively weak potential criticism arises if the foot-specific medication-state response was not incorporated into the ongoing neurological formulation.

### 2019–2021
A stronger question arises as the case developed a difficult benefit-toxicity balance without clear evidence of renewed consultant movement-disorders review.

### Later 2023
The strongest potential departure arises if the explicit OFF-related pain and shuffling were not followed by structured review of the full 24-hour dopaminergic regimen and specialist Parkinson's/movement-disorders input.

## 4. Strongest criticism

The strongest professionally supportable criticism is not failure to prescribe levodopa in 2017.

It is that the evolving case may not have been sufficiently re-synthesised once it showed both:

- clinically relevant symptoms at dopaminergic troughs; and
- dyskinesia/behavioural toxicity at higher exposure.

By later 2023 the problem reasonably called for explicit review of medication timing, duration, formulation and treatment distribution within a narrow therapeutic window, with specialist Parkinson's expertise.

## 5. Strongest defence

The treating neurologist's restraint was based on legitimate concerns that subsequently proved clinically real.

The patient developed dyskinesia/chorea and behavioural toxicity, while the foot syndrome also contained genuine structural pathology, pregabalin-responsive pain and a contradictory 2023 medication-withdrawal report.

The case was therefore clinically difficult and mechanistically mixed.

## 6. Specialist referral

**Optional:** 2017 initial presentation.

**Advisable:** late 2017–2018.

**Strongly indicated:** by 2019, and certainly during 2021–2023.

**Professionally required:** specialist Parkinson's expertise was supported by accepted guidance once dyskinesia and motor fluctuations were being actively treated. Whether consultant movement-disorders co-management was itself mandatory requires Irish expert adjudication.

## 7. Causation

Different management might have reduced some period of OFF-related pain, shuffling, stiffness or morning disability.

The evidence does not establish that earlier levodopa or earlier controlled-release Sinemet would probably have prevented the overall pain or disability.

## 8. Overall assessment

### Credible and requires expert adjudication

There is a credible, professionally grounded question about the longitudinal process of reassessment, particularly from 2019 onward and most clearly by later 2023.

There is substantially less support for an allegation based simply on failure to start levodopa earlier.

The principal professional-standard question is:

> whether a treatment strategy originally justified by youth and treatment risk was sufficiently reopened once patient-specific evidence showed both under-treatment at dopaminergic troughs and toxicity at higher exposure, and whether that evolving therapeutic-window problem should have prompted formal movement-disorders specialist re-evaluation.

---

# Five determinative questions for an independent Irish movement-disorders neurologist

1. By 2018–2019, did the combination of suspected PRKN disease, documented lower-limb medication responsiveness, dyskinesia/chorea and behavioural toxicity make renewed consultant movement-disorders input merely advisable, strongly indicated, or necessary to remain within reasonable Irish neurological practice?

2. Once Neurology documented in later 2023 that leg pain and shuffling were OFF-related, what minimum reassessment of dose size, dose interval, adherence, food/absorption, evening dosing, formulation and overnight coverage would a reasonably competent neurologist ordinarily have undertaken?

3. In a patient with low-state pain/stiffness/shuffling but dyskinesia at higher dopaminergic exposure, would a competent movement-disorders neurologist ordinarily conceptualise management as a narrow therapeutic-window problem requiring optimisation of timing and distribution rather than simply limiting total levodopa exposure?

4. What evidential weight should reasonably be given to the 2017–2018 procyclidine withdrawal/rechallenge, the March 2023 ten-day self-withdrawal report, and the later-2023 symptom-specific OFF observations respectively?

5. If a departure in reassessment or specialist involvement were established by later 2023, can one say on the balance of neurological probabilities that different management would have materially reduced pain or mobility impairment before 2025, or would that remain speculative given the structural pathology, pregabalin responsiveness and high dyskinesia/behavioural sensitivity?
