The clinical argument

A synthesis of the clinical argument, presented alongside the strongest counterpoints, independent review, documentary facts and source records.

What the record supports

  • The original caution with dopaminergic treatment was clinically defensible.
  • Structural foot disease was real and remained relevant.
  • Lower-limb and foot symptoms repeatedly showed medication-state relationships.
  • By later 2023, pain and shuffling were being treated as OFF-related phenomena.
  • The case increasingly resembled a narrow therapeutic-window problem.

What the record does not establish

  • That levodopa should necessarily have been started in 2017.
  • That every episode of foot pain was neurological or dystonic.
  • That earlier controlled-release levodopa would certainly have prevented later disability.
  • That any individual clinician breached the standard of care.
  • That consultant movement-disorders co-management was mandatory without expert Irish evidence.
