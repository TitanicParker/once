PROPOSITIONS

Purpose: These are the material clinical and longitudinal propositions raised by the complete record and requiring testing against the documentary facts and, where appropriate, independent movement-disorders expertise. They are not yet findings.

P01 — Presenting lower-limb/foot phenotype

The foot and lower-limb abnormalities documented before and at the first neurological assessment were potentially part of the presenting movement-disorder phenotype, notwithstanding the simultaneous existence of genuine structural foot pathology.

Expert question: 1.

P02 — Neurology's examination strengthened the neurological-foot connection

Neurology's own findings — including the strikingly abnormal gait, dystonic-looking foot posture, rigidity, bradykinesia and minimal tremor — materially strengthened the pre-existing evidence that the lower-limb/foot syndrome required continued neurological consideration rather than simple separation into a Podiatry problem.

Expert question: 2.

P03 — “Not clear” required an active unresolved model

Once structural foot pathology and abnormal neurological posture/tone were both established, the appropriate working proposition was potentially multi-factorial and unresolved, rather than structurally or neurologically exclusive.

The fact that corns/calluses existed did not itself determine why the feet were being abnormally postured, loaded or experienced.

Expert questions: 1–2.

P04 — Movement-disorders expertise became materially relevant

The combination of unusually young onset, atypical symmetrical akinetic-rigid Parkinsonism, lower-limb dystonia, suspected PRKN/PARK2, treatment sensitivity and consequential dopamine-replacement decisions potentially crossed a threshold at which movement-disorders specialist assessment or continuing co-management became clinically material.

Expert question: 3.

P05 — The procyclidine experiment created new patient-specific evidence

The 2017–2018 procyclidine treatment/withdrawal experience generated new within-patient evidence that at least part of the lower-limb/foot syndrome changed with neurological medication state.

This did not establish the precise mechanism or prove that the structural lesion itself was neurological.

Expert question: 4.

P06 — The procyclidine finding should potentially have altered the earlier evidential position

Once foot-specific medication-state modifiability had been observed and later recorded by the treating neurologist, the original “Not clear” position could no longer safely be treated as though the evidential position had remained unchanged.

The question becomes whether that new fact should have remained an active longitudinal premise when related foot symptoms later recurred.

Expert question: 4.

P07 — The original dopaminergic-restraint policy required re-testing as evidence accumulated

The initial decision to defer levodopa/dopamine replacement may have been clinically reasonable when made, but it was a management strategy based on the evidential state then existing.

As new patient-specific evidence accumulated — dystonia, suspected PARK2, treatment responsiveness and foot medication-state effects — the continued policy of dopaminergic restraint potentially required explicit re-evaluation rather than automatic continuation.

Expert question: 5.

P08 — Continued non-testing had a possible informational cost

A carefully observed dopaminergic trial could potentially have served not merely as treatment but as a diagnostic/therapeutic probe of whether the longstanding foot/lower-limb syndrome varied with dopaminergic state.

If such a test was clinically reasonable, continued non-testing potentially forgone management-relevant information.

Expert question: 5.

This proposition should remain especially carefully worded: it is not the claim that levodopa necessarily should have been commenced in 2017.

P09 — The 2021 substitution created a testable therapeutic prediction

When procyclidine was removed and immediate-release Sinemet substituted, the expectation that Sinemet would “more than compensate” created a testable therapeutic prediction.

Given the earlier foot-specific treatment response, adequacy of substitution potentially required assessment of the feet as a distinct symptom domain, not merely general motor function and tolerability.

Expert question: 6.

P10 — Foot-specific adequacy of the 2021 substitution may not have been demonstrated

The record must be tested for whether immediate-release Sinemet was ever shown to reproduce the foot-specific benefit/control associated with the previous procyclidine-treated state.

If that was not demonstrated, subsequent morning and later-day foot recurrence becomes clinically relevant evidence about the adequacy of the replacement regimen.

Expert question: 6.

P11 — Pregabalin response established modifiability, not necessarily mechanism

Pregabalin responsiveness established that a substantial component of the pain burden was pharmacologically modifiable.

It did not by itself establish peripheral neuropathy as the sole pain generator, particularly where motor/postural symptoms persisted and later pain also varied with dopaminergic state.

Expert question: 7.

P12 — Pregabalin's initiation rationale and later management function must be separated

The contemporaneous reason pregabalin was started must be distinguished from any later role it acquired in management.

A later description of pregabalin as partly levodopa-sparing does not automatically establish that this was its original documented rationale.

Expert question: 7.

P13 — By 2022–2024 the treatment problem potentially concerned dopaminergic distribution

The emerging combination of morning warm-up, later-day deterioration, OFF-associated pain/shuffling, dyskinesia at higher dopaminergic states and persistent postural phenomena potentially raised a treatment problem involving timing, interval, consistency, absorption, formulation and duration of coverage, rather than merely total daily levodopa dose.

Expert question: 8.

P14 — A narrow therapeutic-window model became clinically plausible

The longitudinal evidence potentially supported a model in which:

low dopaminergic states permitted pain, dystonia, rigidity, shuffling or abnormal loading;
an intermediate state improved movement and pain; and
higher states produced dyskinesia.

This is a proposition for expert testing, not an established mechanism.

Expert questions: 8 and 10.

P15 — Later 2023 was a potential global re-synthesis point

Once Neurology itself documented worsening pain and shuffling when Sinemet had not been taken and characterised the state as OFF-related/undertreated, the cumulative evidence potentially required explicit re-synthesis of the earlier foot, dystonia, medication-response, structural, pregabalin and dyskinesia evidence.

Expert question: 9.

P16 — Medication-state sensitivity potentially should have become a durable longitudinal premise

By later 2023, the recurring medication-state observations may have been sufficient that foot/lower-limb medication sensitivity should no longer have functioned merely as a series of local observations, but as an active premise in subsequent treatment architecture.

Expert question: 9.

P17 — The 2025 Sinemet CR response strongly informs the then-current overnight-coverage question

The reported profound response to overnight controlled-release Sinemet provides strong patient-specific evidence that inadequate temporal dopaminergic coverage, particularly the overnight trough, was materially contributing to the then-current morning foot-pain burden.

It does not by itself prove the exact pain generator or establish that every historical foot-pain episode had the same mechanism.

Expert question: 10.

P18 — The 2025 response may retrospectively strengthen, but cannot back-prove, earlier clues

The Sinemet CR natural experiment may legitimately increase the evidential weight of the 2022 morning warm-up phenomenon, later-2023 OFF evidence, the 2021 substitution question and earlier medication-state observations.

It cannot establish that Sinemet CR would necessarily have produced the same result if introduced years earlier.

Expert question: 10.

Longitudinal propositions

P19 — Management strategy should be treated as revisable

A longitudinal treatment plan should potentially be understood as a hypothesis responsive to accumulating patient-specific evidence rather than as a fixed framework into which later evidence is merely accommodated.

Overarching expert question.

P20 — Dopaminergic restraint required repeated risk-benefit re-evaluation

The relevant longitudinal question is not whether the original caution was justified, but whether its continued governing force remained proportionate as evidence of both benefit and risk accumulated.

Overarching expert question.

P21 — There may have been an evidential carry-forward asymmetry

The record may show stronger longitudinal preservation of treatment-risk evidence — dyskinesia, behavioural effects and levodopa sensitivity — than of earlier patient-specific evidence of foot/lower-limb treatment responsiveness.

Whether that asymmetry was clinically appropriate is an expert question.

Overarching expert question.

P22 — Specialist input and model revision are linked

Where unresolved phenotype, treatment sensitivity and suspected monogenic Parkinsonism were directly influencing major treatment decisions, the adequacy of specialist movement-disorders involvement cannot be separated from the adequacy of longitudinal model revision.

Expert question: 3 and overarching question.
